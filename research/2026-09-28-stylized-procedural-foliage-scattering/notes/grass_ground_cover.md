# Stylized painterly grass and ground cover (lawn, meadow, dune grass, reeds) for Godot 4.7

Context read first: `reports/Stylized procedural trees for Godot.md` (incl. Addendum 2026-09-28), `FarmGameGodot/shaders/tree.gdshaderinc`, `FarmGameGodot/shaders/farm_toon.gdshaderinc`. Relevant facts from the existing pipeline that grass must match:
- `light()` = N·L × ATTENUATION → smoothstep band around `shadow_threshold` (0.42–0.45) jittered by world-space value-noise brush (`tr_brush`/`ft_brush`), `DIFFUSE_LIGHT += mix(shadow_tint, 1, band) * LIGHT_COLOR/PI`; tree shadow tint (0.44, 0.56, 0.84), toon default (0.55, 0.53, 0.72); warm highlight cap; back-light translucency `pow(dot(VIEW,-LIGHT),3)`; band-gated rim. `SPECULAR = 0`, `ROUGHNESS = 1`.
- Tree canopy colours: `col_top (0.56,0.71,0.31)`, `col_mid (0.32,0.55,0.30)`, `col_under (0.12,0.32,0.36)`.
- Proxy normals carried in `COLOR.rgb` as `n*0.5+0.5` (Godot axes), gradient in `COLOR.a`; wind = `wind_dir * wind_strength * pow(h,1.8) * sway`, phase from `NODE_POSITION_WORLD` and low-frequency world noise.
- Terrain (`farm_toon` with `use_vertex_color`) colours zones (sand / dune / grass) by **vertex colour in raw sRGB** — there is currently no terrain colour *texture* for grass to sample (this matters for colour-blending, see Q2).
- Addendum lesson: the beach sun sits behind the default camera → front-lit flatness; separation must come from albedo, not lighting. This applies even more to grass.

Labels used below: **[sourced]** = backed by the cited link; **[judgement]** = engineering judgement / inference by this researcher.

## Q1. Construction options at a 45° camera, and what shipped games actually do

### Takeaway
Stylized games with a strong "blades" read (BotW, Ghost of Tsushima, Genshin-likes) use real, GPU-instanced blade geometry near the camera and hand off to **painted terrain colour at distance**; alpha cards/billboard clusters are the older, cheaper alternative and remain the right choice for fluffy painterly clumps; shells suit only very short fur-like cover. For a fixed 45° camera at 5–40 m, the winning combo is **instanced low-poly clump/tuft meshes (opaque, no alpha) near, fading into a terrain shader that paints the grass colour and brush-texture at distance** [judgement, based on the sources below].

### Cited Findings
- Survey of techniques: terrain texturing ("grass is simply textured onto the terrain"), billboards ("render an image of multiple stalks of grass onto a plane" — "the oldest and still a very common way"), and full blade geometry. BotW is cited as the example of geometry grass suiting a "cartoon art style" where "grass blades can be quite tall and plainly colored"; Ghost of Tsushima as "probably the state-of-the-art of realistic (even if somewhat painterly) geometry-based grass" — [hexaquo Grass Rendering Series Pt 1](https://hexaquo.at/pages/grass-rendering-series-part-1-theory/) (Godot-focused series).
- Shell texturing = render multiple offset copies of the surface along the normal to fake many strands; best for short fur/grass — [80.lv summary of Acerola-style explainer](https://80.lv/articles/classic-video-games-trick-for-rendering-grass-fur).
- **Ghost of Tsushima (GDC 2021, Eric Wohllaib, "Procedural Grass in Ghost of Tsushima")**: individual blades generated on the GPU, each with procedural appearance and animation; tiles drawn with instanced indexed draws with no vertex streams (data derived from index/instance ID); cubic Bézier blades; Voronoi-style clumps drive height/facing — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027033/Advanced-Graphics-Summit-Procedural-Grass); summaries at [gist.ly](https://gist.ly/youtube-summarizer/procedural-grass-systems-in-ghost-of-tsushima-achieving-art-direction) and [tigerabrodi.blog](https://tigerabrodi.blog/grass-in-ghost-of-tsushima). Secondary summaries (not the slides) report: near LOD 15 verts/blade, far LOD 7 verts/blade, "very far" replaced by a texture on terrain; LOD transition blends vertex positions to avoid pops; going to larger tiles drops 3 of 4 blades; far tiles use "artist-authored textures"; shadows via impostors rather than full blade shadow pass — [tigerabrodi.blog](https://tigerabrodi.blog/grass-in-ghost-of-tsushima), [gist.ly](https://gist.ly/youtube-summarizer/procedural-grass-systems-in-ghost-of-tsushima-achieving-art-direction). (Flag: these are third-party summaries of the talk; the vertex counts are widely repeated but I could not open the slides.)
- **BotW**: widely analysed as single tapered blade geometry with a vertex-colour root-to-tip gradient (bottom verts black, mid grey, tip white) driving a base→tip colour lerp, little texture, sine/time wind strongest at the tip — [Daniel Ilett, "Stylised Grass with Shaders in URP"](https://danielilett.com/2021-08-24-tut5-17-stylised-grass/) (reverse-engineered, not Nintendo-sourced); BotW's wind system affects objects by weight, grass most prominently — [ResetEra technical analysis](https://www.resetera.com/threads/zelda-breath-of-the-wild-the-technical-analysis.8197/) (fan analysis).
- **Genshin Impact**: no primary miHoYo source found. Community reverse-engineering proposes camera-following grids of quads with IDs in vertex colour, billboard/sway in vertex shader, **atlas tile chosen by the terrain texture underneath**, distant grass as terrain geometry "scaled along the normal" — [Unity Discussions thread](https://discussions.unity.com/threads/genshin-impact-grass-rendering-how.984026/) (speculative). A Stylized Station video claims vertex-shader-driven random placement/bend with noise and a second pass with normal offset for an ink outline — [Class Central listing](https://www.classcentral.com/course/youtube-how-genshin-impact-grass-is-made-and-how-you-can-make-it-too-413152) (not verified against the game).
- **A Short Hike**: the "grass" is largely the terrain shader — texture chosen by terrain normal (up = grass, sides = rock), world-position mapped, splatmap reduced to highest channel for sharp edges; look finished by low-res render + colour-grading post — [Adam Robinson-Yu thread via threadreader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1113100182655262721.html). This is the "shader-only ground" option shipped in a beloved cozy game.
- **Sea of Thieves**: no grass-specific technical details found; the GDC 2018 art talk's rules (simplify structures, hand-paint textures, avoid photo detail) apply generally — [80.lv GDC18 recap](https://80.lv/articles/gdc18-visual-adventures-on-sea-of-thieves), [Habrador summary](https://blog.habrador.com/2018/08/stylized-graphics-fortnite-sea-of-thieves.html).
- **Ghibli-inspired indie/UE projects**: "Meadows" (UE4) used hand-painted grass **cards** cut into clusters, based on Jess Hider's method, plus a large-scale blurry colour-variation texture that made grass "look fluffier and painterly"; the author tried virtual textures for terrain colour, then simplified to a large-scale texture — [80.lv Meadows](https://80.lv/articles/meadows-creating-stylized-nature-in-ue4). Jess Hider's method: masked two-sided foliage with normals forced to world Z-up — [Jess's UE4 Tutorials](https://jesshiderue4.wordpress.com/materials/stylized-wind-blown-grass/). Other 80.lv Ghibli breakdowns report the "biggest breakthrough" as forcing grass card normals to face upward, and using RVT to transfer landscape layer colour to grass — [80.lv search surface: Ghibli island / Bird House / Giant's End](https://80.lv/articles/creating-a-ghibli-inspired-island-in-blender-ue5-substance-3d).
- Blender anime-grass pipeline: 16-triangle low-poly grass, pivot at root, scattered with Distribute Points on Faces + Instance on Points, random Z-rotation and scale — [Trung Duy Nguyen](https://trungduyng.substack.com/p/anime-grass-tutorial-blender).
- Sparse low-poly stylized clumps are typically 20–60 triangles — [Tripo3D asset description](https://studio.tripo3d.ai/3d-model/a-single-low-poly-grass-clump-for-a-stylized-game-environment-sparse-fa2ece0a-a4f0-49e8-868d-eca323f72097) (weak source; indicative only).
- Sable, Tunic: no grass-specific technical source found (Tunic's early art was flat colour on low poly, per the tree report).

### Inferences
- [judgement] At a 45° down-looking camera, individual blades are seen largely **from above**: thin blades show little area and alias into "hair" noise past ~15 m, while top-down coverage comes mostly from the ground colour. So the terrain colour carries the lawn read; geometry adds silhouette, motion and parallax near the camera and at edges (paths, fences, dune crests). This argues for *fewer, chunkier* tufts rather than BotW/GoT-density blades.
- [judgement] Option ranking for this project:
  | Option | Wins when | Loses when |
  |---|---|---|
  | Opaque blade tufts (3–9 tapered blades per mesh, 20–60 tris) via MultiMesh | Lawn/meadow near band 5–20 m; clean toon banding, no overdraw, casts optional shadow | Very dense far fields (sub-pixel noise) |
  | Alpha cards / painted clusters | Fluffy painterly clumps, flower heads, reed seedheads; matches the tree card fringe (same dab atlas family) | Overdraw at density; edge noise at distance |
  | Shells | Very short turf/moss (<5 cm), putting-green lawns | Anything with silhouette; 45° view exposes layer stacks at slopes |
  | Shader-only ground | >20–30 m, and as the always-on base under all geometry | Close-up silhouettes |
- [judgement] Keep geometry **opaque** (no alpha) wherever possible: opaque tufts avoid the alpha-scissor shimmering that the tree card fringe needed `ALPHA_ANTIALIASING_EDGE` for, and are cheaper in depth prepass.

### Gaps
- No primary source (slides/official blog) for BotW, Genshin, Sable, Tunic or Sea of Thieves grass; all game-specific claims except A Short Hike (developer thread) and GoT (GDC Vault abstract + secondary summaries) are reverse-engineering.
- Could not open the GoT slide deck to confirm 15/7 vertex counts or tile sizes.

## Q2. Normals, colour gradients and terrain-colour blending in a custom toon `light()`

### Takeaway
Stylized grass almost universally **overrides blade normals with the ground/up normal** (optionally with a small outward "rounding" tilt near the camera), gets its variation from a root→tip colour gradient plus large-scale colour noise, and blends its root colour into the terrain colour underneath. This plugs straight into the existing painterly `light()`: with up/terrain normals the brush-noise terminator and cast-shadow bands fall on grass exactly as on the ground, so grass and terrain read as one painted surface.

### Cited Findings
- Up-facing normals: Jess Hider's stylized grass disables tangent-space normals and uses world Z-up normals — [Jess's UE4 Tutorials](https://jesshiderue4.wordpress.com/materials/stylized-wind-blown-grass/); Polycount thread on Witness/Fortnite-style grass with upward normals — [Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/219678/grass-normals-issue); 80.lv Ghibli breakdowns: forcing card normals up "eliminat[es] any unwanted shadows or unappealing shading" — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/creating-a-ghibli-inspired-island-in-blender-ue5-substance-3d).
- Godot CC0 "Stylized MultiMesh Grass" shader "aligns the normals of the mesh facing upwards no matter the original direction", bottom/top colour mix on UV.y, Perlin FBM wind, player-position push — [godotshaders (CC0)](https://godotshaders.com/shader/stylized-multimesh-grass-shader/) (comments reference Godot 4.5).
- A commercial Godot 4 grass (EmacEArt 1.2.0) diagnosed "light and dark flicker" at distance as blades catching light edge-on; fix was to make blades "face the sky more" so "the field keeps one steady tone at any distance" — [EmacEArt devlog](https://emaceart.itch.io/emaceart-stylized-grass-grass-shader-for-godot-4/devlog/1646260/emaceart-stylized-grass-120-godot-4-grass-shader-without-the-light-and-dark-flicker).
- GoT-style rounding: normals tilted outward across the blade width for a rounded look without extra verts, and **blended toward the terrain normal with distance** to reduce aliasing — [cainrademan/Unity-Grass README](https://github.com/cainrademan/Unity-Grass) (attributes this to GoT; licence "not intended for commercial use"), [tigerabrodi.blog](https://tigerabrodi.blog/grass-in-ghost-of-tsushima). GodotGrass (2Retr0, MIT) does "a rounder appearance ... by bending the normals horizontally in the fragment shader" — [GitHub](https://github.com/2Retr0/GodotGrass).
- GoT view-space thickening: when a blade is seen edge-on, verts are rotated about the blade tangent so it doesn't vanish — [Unity-Grass](https://github.com/cainrademan/Unity-Grass).
- Root→tip gradient: BotW-analysis vertex-colour gradient with base and tip colours — [Daniel Ilett](https://danielilett.com/2021-08-24-tut5-17-stylised-grass/); godotshaders "Stylized Cartoon Grass" (MIT, Godot 4.x) samples a `terrain_color` for the root and `grass_color` for the top and blends with a gradient mask — [godotshaders](https://godotshaders.com/shader/stylized-cartoon-grass/); GDQuest-derived shader uses a colour-ramp texture on `1-UV.y` — [godotshaders](https://godotshaders.com/shader/stylized-grass-with-wind-and-deformation/).
- Terrain colour transfer: UE projects use Runtime Virtual Texturing to paint grass with landscape layer colours — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/creating-a-ghibli-inspired-island-in-blender-ue5-substance-3d); Meadows replaced RVT with a single large-scale blurry colour-variation texture — [80.lv Meadows](https://80.lv/articles/meadows-creating-stylized-nature-in-ue4). GoT colours derive from terrain texture sampling plus artist data — [tigerabrodi.blog](https://tigerabrodi.blog/grass-in-ghost-of-tsushima) (secondary).
- Wind-coupled colour: the anime-grass Blender pipeline stores the wind pattern as a named attribute and darkens colour where wind bends grass, synced to motion — [Trung Duy Nguyen](https://trungduyng.substack.com/p/anime-grass-tutorial-blender). hexaquo darkens AO with bend: `AO = bottom_to_top - current_wind_bend * wind_ao_affect` — [hexaquo Pt 3](https://hexaquo.at/pages/grass-rendering-series-part-3-animating-and-interacting-with-grass-in-godot/).
- Godot per-instance uniforms "do not support textures or arrays", max 16 per shader — [Godot shading language docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/shaders/shader_reference/shading_language.html).

### Inferences
- [judgement] Concrete Godot mapping for our `light()`:
  - In `vertex()`, `NORMAL` is model-space; for MultiMesh the instance transform is in `MODEL_MATRIX`. Set `vec3 up_m = normalize((inverse(MODEL_MATRIX) * vec4(ground_n_world, 0.0)).xyz); NORMAL = normalize(mix(up_m, NORMAL_rounded, round_amt * near_fade));` with `round_amt` ≈ 0.15–0.3 and `near_fade` → 0 by ~12 m. `ground_n_world` = (0,1,0) on flat lawn, or the terrain normal stored per instance (see Q6) on dunes. This is the grass equivalent of the tree's `spherize` proxy.
  - Keep `world_pos` from the **blade root / instance origin** (not the vertex) for the brush-noise term: `tr_brush(root_world_pos)` makes each tuft take one band value like the ground under it, and the terminator reads as the same painted stroke crossing grass and ground. Use the **same `brush_scale` and `shadow_threshold` as the terrain material** so the band is continuous.
  - Translucency: reuse the tree's back-light term, but weight it by tip gradient (`h^2`), so sunlit tips glow yellow-green when the camera looks into the sun. With the beach sun behind the camera this term rarely fires; the colour gradient must carry the look (Addendum lesson 4).
  - Rim: set `rim_intensity` ≈ 0 for grass; rim on up-normals is meaningless and on rounded normals it fizzes.
  - `SPECULAR = 0`, `ROUGHNESS = 1`, `AO_LIGHT_AFFECT = 0` as in tree.gdshaderinc; drive `AO` from the height gradient (root 0.4–0.6 → tip 1.0).
- [judgement] Colour recipe: `albedo = mix(terrain_col_at_root, grass_base, smoothstep(0.0, 0.35, h))` then `mix(→ grass_tip, h)`; multiply by a low-frequency world-space colour-variation noise (2–8 m period, ±5–8 % value, a few degrees hue — matches the tree `color_jitter` philosophy) and per-clump jitter. Suggested starting palette to sit next to the tree colours: lawn base ≈ tree `col_mid` (0.32,0.55,0.30), tip ≈ tree `col_top` (0.56,0.71,0.31); dune grass base (0.42,0.50,0.33) → tip (0.78,0.74,0.50) straw; reeds (0.30,0.46,0.30) → (0.62,0.62,0.38). These are starting values only.
- [judgement] Terrain colour source for this project: the terrain uses vertex colours, which grass instances cannot sample in a shader. Cheapest robust path: **sample the terrain vertex colour at scatter time and write it into MultiMesh per-instance `COLOR`** (sRGB→linear as `farm_toon` does). Alternative: bake a top-down terrain colour texture (orthographic render or Blender bake) and pass it as a material uniform `sampler2D` with world→UV mapping; this also gives the "shader-only far ground" a matching texture.

### Gaps
- No source measuring BACKLIGHT/translucency behaviour under an overridden `light()` in Godot 4.7 (same gap as in the tree report).
- No first-party Ghibli/AWL/Ni no Kuni grass shading reference; the upward-normal consensus comes from indie/UE breakdowns.

## Q3. Wind and player interaction

### Takeaway
Use a **scrolling world-space noise texture** (ridged/simplex) for gust bands on top of a small per-blade sine bob; share the same wind uniforms as the trees. For interaction, a **per-material uniform array of a few positions** is the cheapest and simplest for a single player plus a few NPCs/animals; a **top-down trail render texture** only earns its cost if you want persistent flattened paths. Both are cheap on a 5070 Ti; the main cost risk is vertex count, not wind.

### Cited Findings
- Ghibli-style rolling gusts: "When observing a large field of grass with wind, you notice a similarity to waves as gusts move through it... Wind strongly bends and shakes grass where it is currently strongest; once it has passed, the stalks stand mostly upright and shake only lightly"; recommended **NoiseTexture2D, Simplex Smooth, fractal type Ridged**, fractal gain controlling small-scale shake; sampled at `NODE_POSITION_WORLD.xz`/world position scrolled by `TIME * wind_direction * wind_strength`; bend scaled by `bottom_to_top`; wind direction transformed to model space with `inverse(MODEL_MATRIX)`; code under CC-BY-SA — [hexaquo Pt 3](https://hexaquo.at/pages/grass-rendering-series-part-3-animating-and-interacting-with-grass-in-godot/).
- GoT wind: 2D Perlin noise scrolled directionally, shared CPU/GPU, plus per-blade sine bobbing with position-based phase; wind also affects blade facing — [gist.ly](https://gist.ly/youtube-summarizer/procedural-grass-systems-in-ghost-of-tsushima-achieving-art-direction), [tigerabrodi.blog](https://tigerabrodi.blog/grass-in-ghost-of-tsushima). GodotGrass adds multi-frequency Perlin and simplex domain warp for turbulence — [GitHub](https://github.com/2Retr0/GodotGrass).
- Ghibli-inspired UE island used "wind-lines" details giving "a rippling glow when wind blows" — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/creating-a-ghibli-inspired-island-in-blender-ue5-substance-3d). Three-layer wind (whole clump sway, blade tremble, gusts) in a Godot 4 commercial grass — [EmacEArt devlog](https://emaceart.itch.io/emaceart-stylized-grass-grass-shader-for-godot-4/devlog/1646260/emaceart-stylized-grass-120-grass-shader-for-godot-4-now-with-a-40-second-trailer).
- Interaction via distance falloff: `bend_away_strength = max(object_radius - object_distance, 0.0) / object_radius`, positions pushed via `set_shader_parameter("object_position", ...)` from a script on the MultiMesh; "cutting" by clamping `VERTEX.y` — [hexaquo Pt 3](https://hexaquo.at/pages/grass-rendering-series-part-3-animating-and-interacting-with-grass-in-godot/). GDQuest-derived shader (MIT) supports one character because "you can't export an array of varying sizes" (a **Godot 3.2** limitation; comments report texture-repeat issues on 4.1.3+) — [godotshaders](https://godotshaders.com/shader/stylized-grass-with-wind-and-deformation/). A Godot forum shader supports up to 10 bending objects — [Godot Forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/updated-simple-grass-shader-to-support-bending/129115).
- Godot 4 supports uniform arrays; `uniform vec3 positions[N]` must be set with a `PackedVector3Array` or it silently fails — [Bugnet blog](https://bugnet.io/blog/fix-godot-shader-uniform-array-not-updating); global uniforms cannot be arrays (open proposal) — [godot-proposals #9553](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-proposals/discussions/9553).
- Global uniforms are available in all shader types — [Godot shading language docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/shaders/shader_reference/shading_language.html). Global `sampler2D` uniforms exist (Project Settings → Shader Globals) but have a history of bugs: blank/white textures ([#107470](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/107470)), not applied at runtime ([#70649](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/70649)), not saved when anonymous in 4.3 beta ([#93164](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/93164)), not updating in 4.1.1 ([#80558](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/80558)). Save the texture resource to a file first as the workaround ([#93164](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/93164)).
- SimpleGrassTextured interaction: characters carry a hidden MeshInstance3D (typically a sphere) on render layer 17, excluded from the main camera, which the plugin renders to derive deformation; enabled with `SimpleGrass.set_interactive(true)`; baking the heightmap speeds loading — [README](https://github.com/IcterusGames/SimpleGrassTextured/blob/main/README.md). (i.e. a render-texture trail approach.)
- GoT interaction: a displacement buffer of character/object positions consumed on GPU — [tigerabrodi.blog](https://tigerabrodi.blog/grass-in-ghost-of-tsushima) (secondary).

### Inferences
- [judgement] Wind layering to match the trees: promote the tree shader's `wind_dir`/`wind_speed`/`wind_strength` to **`global uniform`s** (vec2/float are safe types) so grass and trees gust together; the grass reads the same scrolling gust noise at the blade root, and trees can sample it at `NODE_POSITION_WORLD` to lean when a gust band passes. Layers: (1) gust bend = ridged noise(root.xz·0.05–0.1 − t·dir·speed) × h^1.5–2; (2) per-clump sway sine with phase from clump id; (3) tip flutter small sine per blade. Keep amplitudes cozy: lawn tip ≤ 5–8 cm, meadow ≤ 20–30 cm in gusts, dune grass larger and "combed" (see Q5), reeds pivot from base with slow long-period sway.
- [judgement] Ghibli "rolling wave" readability comes from **colour** as much as motion: brighten/desaturate slightly where gust noise is high (grass blades turning their paler undersides/sheen toward the camera). Add `albedo *= 1 + gust * 0.08` and optional tip-highlight lift — the "wind-lines rippling glow" of the 80.lv Ghibli island. At a 45° camera the colour wave is more visible than the bend.
- [judgement] Interaction choice: `uniform vec4 pushers[8]` (xyz position, w radius) on the grass material(s), updated once per frame from one autoload (`RenderingServer.material_set_param` or `set_shader_parameter` on the shared material) — O(8) per vertex, negligible. Use a trail render-texture (SubViewport, top-down orthographic, R/G = bend direction, B = age, fades over seconds) only if persistent footpaths in meadows are wanted; route it as a per-material `sampler2D` rather than a global to avoid the global-sampler bugs above, or verify those bugs are fixed on 4.7 first.
- [judgement] Cost: all of the above are a handful of ALU ops + 1 texture fetch per vertex; with ~20–60 tri tufts even 200k visible instances is a few million verts, which a 5070 Ti handles comfortably. Shadows (Q4) are the real cost lever.

### Gaps
- No measured Godot numbers for trail-texture vs uniform-array interaction cost.
- Could not confirm whether the global `sampler2D` bugs listed are all fixed in 4.7.

## Q4. Godot 4 specifics: MultiMesh vs GPUParticles3D, chunking, visibility ranges, budgets, existing projects

### Takeaway
Use **chunked MultiMeshInstance3D** (≈16–32 m cells), generated offline/at load from the terrain, with per-instance COLOR/CUSTOM data, `visibility_range_end` + dither fade per chunk, and **shadows off** for short grass. GPUParticles3D is viable for camera-following infinite grass but is less controllable and reportedly slower per instance; for a hand-built village map, MultiMesh wins. Reuse ideas (not wholesale code) from the MIT/CC0 projects below; none are fully compatible with our custom `light()` out of the box.

### Cited Findings
- MultiMesh: "There is no screen or frustum culling possible for individual instances"; docs recommend several MultiMeshes for different areas; `visible_instance_count` lets you allocate max and show fewer; per-instance data via `INSTANCE_ID`/`INSTANCE_CUSTOM`; `RenderingServer.multimesh_set_buffer()` for fast bulk upload (docs page is the 4.7/latest version) — [Godot docs, Using MultiMesh](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/performance/using_multimesh.html).
- Terrain3D (MIT, C++ GDExtension) instancer: regions split into **32×32 m cells**, each its own MultiMesh for frustum/occlusion culling; up to 10 LODs (4 recommended) with visible ranges defaulting to 32 m spacing; LOD switch per cell; `last_shadow_lod` and `shadow_impostor` options; also ships a **particle shader that generates grass around the camera** (`extras/particle_example`); no collision for instances — [Terrain3D docs 1.1](https://terrain3d.readthedocs.io/en/latest/docs/instancer.html), [licence](https://godotengine.org/asset-library/asset/3892).
- MultiMesh vs GPUParticles3D: forum report of 1M MultiMesh instances at 144 fps vs 1M particles at ~1 fps (anecdotal; engine version unclear) — [Godot Forum archive](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/particle-grass-vs-multimesh-grass-which-performs-better/5261); a Godot proposal discusses indirect rendering / transform-less MultiMesh for this use case — [godot-proposals #8647](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-proposals/discussions/8647).
- hexaquo Godot LOD test: 10,000 blades per 5×5 m chunk (**400 blades/m²**) at 9 tris/blade = 90k tris/chunk; 50×50 m (100 chunks, ~10M tris) rendered in "just under 2 ms" on the author's machine; high-LOD geometry fades out 10→20 m while an impostor plane fades in 5→10 m; notes sub-pixel geometry causes flicker and noise — [hexaquo Pt 4](https://hexaquo.at/pages/grass-rendering-series-part-4-level-of-detail-tricks-for-infinite-plains-of-grass-in-godot/).

### Inferences
- [judgement] Chunk size 16 m for lawn/meadow (small village, fine-grained culling at a 45° camera whose footprint is roughly 40–60 m deep), 32 m for sparse dune grass and reeds. Put each chunk's origin at its centre (SimpleGrassTextured's "auto center" advice is the same point: AABB/visibility distance is measured from the node).
- [judgement] Per chunk: `visibility_range_end` = 28–35 m (lawn tufts), 45–60 m (meadow, dune grass, reeds — taller silhouettes matter further out), `visibility_range_end_margin` 4–6 m, `visibility_range_fade_mode = SELF` (dither). Additionally shrink tufts in the vertex shader over the last ~30 % of range (scale Y toward 0 as distance → end) so they sink into the ground colour instead of dithering — GoT's "blend vertex positions" idea and hexaquo's fade-to-ground combined.
- [judgement] Density budgets (starting points for a 5070 Ti at 1440p, to measure): lawn 60–120 tufts/m² near band (each 5–7 blades, ~30–50 tris) ≈ 300–800 blades/m² equivalent, comparable to hexaquo's 400 blades/m²; meadow 25–50 tall tufts/m² in patches; dune grass 0.5–3 clumps/m² in drifts; reeds 5–15 clumps/m² along water edges only. With chunk culling, visible grass should stay under ~5–10M tris; profile with the Godot profiler / RenderDoc.
- [judgement] Shadows: `cast_shadow = OFF` for lawn and meadow (grass receives shadows from trees/buildings via ATTENUATION, which our `light()` already bands). Dune grass and reeds: consider `cast_shadow = ON` only for the nearest chunk ring, or a `SHADOWS_ONLY` low-poly proxy, because their shadows on bright sand are a strong cozy-beach cue. GodotGrass notes the "very high" cost of grass shadows.
- [judgement] MultiMesh setup: `transform_format = 3D`, `use_colors = true` (terrain colour at root, sRGB→linear in shader or pre-linearised), `use_custom_data = true` (x = clump id/phase, y = height scale/type, z/w = terrain normal xz). Generate chunks in a `@tool` GDScript from terrain raycasts or directly in Blender (see Q6), saving `.tres` MultiMesh resources so runtime cost is zero.
- [judgement] GPUParticles3D camera-following grass (Terrain3D's approach) is worth it only for large open terrain; our fixed-camera village with hand-authored zones benefits more from painted/deterministic MultiMesh placement.
- [judgement] Instance uniforms do not vary per MultiMesh instance (same conclusion as the tree report); use `INSTANCE_CUSTOM`/`COLOR`.

## Q5. Beach/dune grass (marram) and reeds/rushes: how they differ from lawn grass

### Takeaway
Dune grass and reeds are **sparse, silhouette-driven clump plants**, not carpets: they should be modelled as distinct clumps (radiating or vertical fans) scattered in drifts with sand/water visible between them, animated with larger, slower sway pivoting at the base, and given more colour contrast (straw tips) than lawn grass. Lawn and meadow are **density-driven** carpets that lean on terrain colour.

### Cited Findings
- Marram grass (*Ammophila arenaria*) grows on coastal sand dunes, in clumps, singly, with or without flowering spikes; it prefers active sand on the windward side of the foredune — [Wikipedia, Ammophila arenaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammophila_arenaria), [Daz3D product text](https://www.daz3d.com/just-beachy--marram-and-dune-grass-for-daz-studio).
- Ecology: marram's spatial organization (clumps vs patches) steers dune development via biomorphogenic feedback — [Frontiers in Ecology & Evolution 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2021.761336/full). (Useful as a placement rule: clumps colonise crests and windward faces, gaps and blowouts between.)
- Sparse stylized dune clumps: "only a few thin blades emerging from a small patch of dry soil", 20–60 triangles, "no individual detailed blades" — [Tripo3D asset text](https://studio.tripo3d.ai/3d-model/a-single-low-poly-grass-clump-for-a-stylized-game-environment-sparse-fa2ece0a-a4f0-49e8-868d-eca323f72097) (weak source). Instancing with slight scale and Y-rotation variation "to make one plant look like hundreds" — [Daz3D product text](https://www.daz3d.com/just-beachy--marram-and-dune-grass-for-daz-studio).
- Reeds/cattails (*Typha*): tall, slender leaves with dense cylindrical flower spikes — [Dreamstime description](https://www.dreamstime.com/illustration/cattail-silhouette.html) (stock illustration; weak).
- I found **no** technical game-art source specific to stylized dune grass or reed rendering.

### Inferences
- [judgement] **Dune grass silhouette**: a hemispherical "fountain" of 12–30 long, narrow, arching blades (0.5–1.0 m), leaves rolled so they read as thin strokes; tips curved outward and often wind-combed to leeward; occasional straight pale seed spikes (1–3 per clump) poking above. Clump base ~0.2–0.4 m wide; slight sand mound at base (a darker AO ring painted into the terrain colour, or a tiny sand-coloured cone at the root).
- [judgement] **Dune grass scattering**: Poisson-disk clumps in drifts driven by a low-frequency mask (dune crest/windward face bias from terrain slope & normal), with **large gaps of bare sand**; density falls to zero toward the wet-sand line. Per-clump lean baked toward the prevailing sea wind (constant 5–15° lean plus wind sway on top) — a "combed" dune look reads instantly as coastal.
- [judgement] **Reeds/rushes silhouette**: strongly vertical, 1.0–2.0 m, 6–15 straight-to-slightly-arched stems/leaves per clump, a few with cattail heads (a cylinder-ish brown blob on a stalk — good as a small opaque mesh, no alpha). Place in dense bands along pond/stream edges and shallow water, following the shoreline curve; thin to nothing within 1–3 m inland. Wind: rigid stems pivoting from base (rotate about root, not bend), slower period (2–4 s), phase offset along the bank so waves travel along the band; cattail heads bob slightly.
- [judgement] **Normals**: lawn/meadow → up/terrain normal (Q2). Dune grass and reeds are tall enough that a pure up-normal flattens their silhouette at 5–15 m; use a **per-clump spherical proxy normal** (exactly the tree canopy trick: `normal = normalize(vertex - clump_centre_raised)` blended ~50–70 % with up) so each clump gets a light side and a teal shadow side like a small bush, matching the trees.
- [judgement] **Colour**: dune grass is paler, yellower and more desaturated than lawn (sun-bleached straw tips, grey-green base); reeds slightly bluer-green at base with warm tan tips/heads. Both need stronger tip-highlight and translucency than lawn because their silhouettes are seen against bright sand/water.

### Gaps
- No studio source on stylized coastal vegetation (e.g. Sea of Thieves islands' beach grass construction) was found.
- No reference for Ghibli/AWL-specific dune grass treatment.

## Q6. Procedural generation of blade/tuft meshes and what to bake into COLOR/UV

### Takeaway
Generate a small **library of tuft meshes** (e.g. 4–8 variants per grass type) in Blender 5.2 Geometry Nodes (same headless pipeline as the trees) with per-vertex data baked into COLOR_0/UV2; do **placement** either in Blender (exporting instance transforms) or in a Godot `@tool` script building chunked MultiMeshes from the terrain. Per-instance data (terrain colour, terrain normal, clump id, phase) goes in MultiMesh COLOR/CUSTOM, not in the mesh.

### Cited Findings
- GoT blades: cubic Bézier curves defining shape and animation; per-blade parameters (height, facing, bend) from Voronoi clump cells ("every blade knows which cell it belongs to") — [tigerabrodi.blog](https://tigerabrodi.blog/grass-in-ghost-of-tsushima), [Unity-Grass](https://github.com/cainrademan/Unity-Grass); vertex redistribution toward tips — [Unity-Grass](https://github.com/cainrademan/Unity-Grass).
- GodotGrass generates positions on CPU into MultiMesh, uses cellular noise for clumping; notes CPU-side blade positions limit dynamic LOD flexibility — [GitHub](https://github.com/2Retr0/GodotGrass).
- Blender anime-grass pipeline: GN Distribute Points on Faces + Instance on Points, random Z rotation 0–π and scale; wind pattern stored as a named attribute for the shader — [Trung Duy Nguyen](https://trungduyng.substack.com/p/anime-grass-tutorial-blender). Ashlee Alaniz's Ghibli-style grass is a GN setup rendered in Eevee — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/fascinating-ghibli-style-grass-setup-crafted-with-geometry-nodes).
- BotW-style vertex colour gradient root→tip — [Daniel Ilett](https://danielilett.com/2021-08-24-tut5-17-stylised-grass/). GDQuest demo generates grass strands with GDScript into a MultiMesh — [godotshaders](https://godotshaders.com/shader/stylized-grass-with-wind-and-deformation/).
- From our own Addendum: FLOAT_COLOR attributes survive Blender 5.2 → glTF → Godot 4.7 (decode error ≤ 0.013) with `force_disable_compression`, `generate_lods` off; any float2 corner attribute exports as a UV map; `Grid to Mesh` needs `Set Shade Smooth` — [local report](../../2026-09-27-stylized-procedural-trees-godot/report.md).
- Crysis-style RGBA wind data in vertex colour (main bend by height, per-part phase, edge flutter) — [GPU Gems 3 ch.16](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iii-rendering/chapter-16-vegetation-procedural-animation-and-shading-crysis) (via tree report).

### Inferences
- [judgement] **Tuft mesh generator (GN, headless, JSON-driven like `gn_tree.py`)**: for each of N blades: pick a root offset inside a disk of radius r (lawn 0.05–0.1 m, dune 0.15–0.3 m, reeds 0.1–0.2 m), a yaw, an outward tilt (lawn 5–25°, dune 20–60° fountain, reeds 0–10°), height from `mix(hmin,hmax,rand^1.5)`, then sweep a Bézier (`Curve Line`/`Bezier Segment` → `Resample Curve` 3–5 points → `Curve to Mesh` with a 2-vertex flat profile, or a custom taper via radius) to get a tapered ribbon of 2–4 segments ending in a single tip vertex. Lawn blade: 3 segments = 7 verts (GoT far-LOD count), 5 tris; dune blade 4 segments; reeds 3–4 segments with occasional cattail head (a 6-sided capsule, ~20 tris). Join, `Set Shade Smooth`, export glTF.
- [judgement] **Per-vertex bake (mesh, COLOR_0 as FLOAT_COLOR)** — mirrors the tree convention so shared include code works:
  - **R** = normalised height along blade `h` (0 root → 1 tip) — drives colour gradient, AO and bend weight.
  - **G** = per-blade random (phase / colour jitter / bend variation).
  - **B** = blade-width coordinate (−1…1 mapped to 0…1) — for GoT-style normal rounding and optional view-space thickening.
  - **A** = stiffness/type mask (e.g. 1 = seed spike or cattail head that should bob rather than bend; 0 = leaf).
  - **UV** = (width coord, h) for an optional painted blade stroke texture; **UV2** = blade root offset from tuft origin (xz) so the shader can bend each blade about its own root and compute per-blade proxy normals. (Alternatively put the clump-proxy normal in COLOR like the tree cards, but for grass up/terrain normals are computed in-shader, so COLOR is freed for the data above.)
- [judgement] **Per-instance (MultiMesh)**: `COLOR` = terrain colour at root (sampled from terrain vertex colour at placement time); `CUSTOM` = (clump id hash, height scale, terrain normal x, terrain normal z) — reconstruct normal y. Instance transform carries yaw + scale + the baked prevailing-wind lean for dunes.
- [judgement] **Placement**: author a per-type density mask (Blender vertex group / attribute painted on the terrain, or a Godot image) and generate in a `@tool` GDScript: Poisson-disk or jittered grid per 16/32 m chunk, reject by mask/slope/"no-grass" zones (paths, farm plots, building footprints), raycast/sample terrain height & colour, write `.tres`. Keeps placement in Godot where gameplay zones live, and makes tilling/cutting a matter of editing the mask and regenerating a chunk. Blender GN placement is fine for a first pass if the terrain is authored there, exported as a points glTF / JSON of transforms.
- [judgement] **Clumping**: GoT's Voronoi clumps map to GN `Voronoi Texture` (cell id + distance) evaluated at instance positions → per-instance height scale, lean toward/away from cell centre, and colour jitter; store the cell id hash in `CUSTOM.x`. This gives the meadow its "patchy" painted look far more cheaply than more density.

### Gaps
- Not tested live (Blender MCP not connected this session); GN node names for Bézier ribbon generation in 5.2 should be introspected before scripting.
- Godot's glTF import mapping of extra attributes to CUSTOM0–3 remains unverified (same gap as the tree report); COLOR_0 + UV/UV2 are the proven channels.

## Q7. Recommended recipe per grass type for this project

### Takeaway
One shared `grass.gdshaderinc` built on the tree/toon `light()` (same brush-noise band, shadow tint, highlight cap, back-light), with `#define`s for LAWN, MEADOW, DUNE, REED; one GN tuft generator; one Godot `@tool` chunk scatterer; terrain colour carried per instance; shared global wind. All numbers below are **[judgement] starting targets** to calibrate in the lookdev loop (front-lit game-camera shot as must-pass, per the Addendum).

### Cited Findings
- Supporting evidence is cited in Q1–Q6: up/terrain normals ([Jess Hider](https://jesshiderue4.wordpress.com/materials/stylized-wind-blown-grass/), [EmacEArt](https://emaceart.itch.io/emaceart-stylized-grass-grass-shader-for-godot-4/devlog/1646260/emaceart-stylized-grass-120-godot-4-grass-shader-without-the-light-and-dark-flicker)), distance handoff to terrain texture ([tigerabrodi on GoT](https://tigerabrodi.blog/grass-in-ghost-of-tsushima), [hexaquo Pt 4](https://hexaquo.at/pages/grass-rendering-series-part-4-level-of-detail-tricks-for-infinite-plains-of-grass-in-godot/)), ridged-noise gusts ([hexaquo Pt 3](https://hexaquo.at/pages/grass-rendering-series-part-3-animating-and-interacting-with-grass-in-godot/)), chunked MultiMesh ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/performance/using_multimesh.html), [Terrain3D](https://terrain3d.readthedocs.io/en/latest/docs/instancer.html)), shadows off for short grass ([SimpleGrassTextured](https://github.com/IcterusGames/SimpleGrassTextured/blob/main/README.md)).

### Inferences
**Shared foundation [judgement]**
- Terrain shader (`farm_toon` painterly) gets a "grass zone" variant: world-space grass-stroke texture/brush dabs + low-frequency colour variation, so the ground alone reads as painted lawn at 25–40 m (A Short Hike approach). This is the far LOD for every type.
- `grass.gdshaderinc`: vertex() — root-relative bend (gust noise × h^1.7 + clump sway + tip flutter + pushers[8]), distance sink (scale toward root over last 30 % of range), normal = mix(terrain-up, rounded/proxy, k·near_fade); fragment() — albedo = terrain colour at root → base → tip gradient × world colour noise × clump jitter × gust sheen; AO from h; opaque, `cull_disabled`. light() — copy of tree `light()` with brush noise at **root world pos**, rim off, translucency weighted by h².
- Globals: `wind_dir`, `wind_speed`, `wind_strength`, `gust_scale` as `global uniform` floats/vec2; gust noise texture as a per-material uniform (avoid global-sampler bugs until verified on 4.7).

**Build order [judgement]**
1. Terrain grass-zone paint (no geometry) — validates the far read and colours.
2. Lawn tufts + shared include + MultiMesh chunk scatterer (`@tool`), terrain colour per instance; check the front-lit game-camera shot for the "one painted surface" read.
3. Global wind + gust sheen; hook trees to the same globals.
4. Dune grass (proxy normals, lean), then reeds, then meadow patches with Voronoi clumping.
5. Interaction (pushers), then profile; only then consider shadows on dune/reed near ring.

**Eval gates (reusing the tree approach) [judgement]**: tri budget per tuft; per-chunk visible instance cap; no "flicker" test = render the 40 m shot at two camera offsets 0.5 m apart and diff luminance of grass regions (band flicker shows as high diff); silhouette check for dune/reed clumps from the 45° pitch.
