# Retail deductions and tax appeals beat R&D credits

As of late September 2026, **the best contingency market for an outcome-priced AI agent is CPG retail deduction recovery, and the worst is the one ranked first before this research: R&D tax credits.** The deciding variable is not how cleanly an outcome can be counted. It is whether the paying side can count it too. Where the payer can detect the error itself, the fee collapses: Amazon now reimburses most FBA losses automatically, and ERP-native tools pre-empt duplicate payments. Where a counterparty resists (a retailer that deducted, a county appraisal district, a carrier), contingency fees hold. **Property tax appeals rank second**, in small and mid-size commercial work done by licensed firms rather than mass residential filing. In Harris County, agents filed 80% of 2025 protests and won only 16% of them, against 71% for homeowners filing themselves ([Square Deal, HCAD data](https://blog.squaredeal.tax/texas/harris-county-protest-data/)). **Inbound lead intake priced per booked appointment ranks third.** The lead-gen market as a whole deserved its last place in the earlier ranking, but this segment did not. **Recruitment ranks fourth.** Its outcome model is proven, but Paraform holds the high-fee lane with human recruiters, and autonomous candidate ranking carries live discrimination liability. **Tax credits rank last as an outcome-priced market.** The IRS keeps raising the evidence bar, and OBBBA's ERC rules define a promoter partly by charging "fee based on refund" ([RSM](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2025/what-you-need-to-know-about-obbba-and-the-employee-retention-tax-credit.html)). A December 2024 Circular 230 proposal would ban contingent fees on returns and refund claims ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/12/26/2024-29371/regulations-governing-practice-before-the-internal-revenue-service)). The best-funded startup, MainStreet, ended as a 15-person acqui-hire ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3003322)). Tax credits are ripe for AI, but at a flat price per evidence file, not a percentage of the credit. In all five markets the winners are marketplaces, licensed firms or platform features with AI inside, and the biggest exits so far are roll-ups.

| Rank | Market | Best segment for a new entrant | Pricing unit | Trap to avoid | Earlier from-memory rank |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recovery audit | CPG retail deductions (freight/parcel audit as runner-up) | % of dollars recovered on disputed deductions | Generic AP duplicate recovery and Amazon FBA, where the payer self-detects | Suggested as a better alternative. **Right market, wrong default segment** |
| 2 | Property tax appeals | Small/mid commercial in annual-notice states, via TDLR-registered or attorney-led firm | Contingency on first-year tax savings, per protested year | Volume filing that collapses win rates | Suggested as a better alternative. **Right** |
| 3 | Lead generation | Inbound speed-to-lead / intake for PI law and home services | Per booked appointment or signed case, in the buyer's own logs | Outbound consumer AI voice (uncapped TCPA damages); vendor-as-verifier | Ranked last. **Right for the market, wrong for this segment** |
| 4 | Agency recruitment | Nursing/allied-health and licensed-trade permanent placement via a recruiter marketplace | Flat fee per start, with 60–90 day guarantee | Autonomous candidate ranking (AEDT, *Mobley*); ownership disputes | Ranked second. **Roughly right, but the model is taken at the top** |
| 5 | R&D and tax credits | Mid-market Section G evidence files, sold through CPA firms and payroll/ledger platforms | Flat fee per claim or per business component, with audit-defense warranty | Percentage-of-credit pricing (ERC promoter definition; Circular 230) | Ranked first. **Wrong** |

## The earlier ranking got the alternatives right and the leader wrong

Before this run, a from-memory opinion ranked tax credits first, recruitment second and lead generation last, and suggested recovery audit and property tax appeals as better alternatives. The evidence supports the two alternatives and the low rank for lead gen as a market. It reverses the top pick. It also shows that three of the five calls depended on choosing a segment that the opinion did not specify.

| Earlier claim | Verdict | What the evidence shows |
|---|---|---|
| Tax credits are the best market | **Wrong** | Fees are compressing from 25–40% (boutiques) to 15–20% (Gusto "up to 15%", Pilot 20%) ([Gusto](https://gusto.com/accountants/r-and-d-tax-credit-service); [Pilot](https://pilot.com/rd-credit)). Verification latency runs about 3 years to audit close. The regulatory direction runs against contingency pricing. The best-funded entrant failed |
| Price tax credits on outcome | **Wrong as a percentage** | A percentage of the refund is the pricing unit regulators single out: the OBBBA ERC promoter test and the proposed Circular 230 ban. A flat fee per defensible file fits the evidence |
| Recruitment is second | **Roughly right** | Paraform proves per-hire AI-era pricing at scale (1,000+ customers, $40M Series B) ([Paraform](https://www.paraform.com/blog/series-b)). But it keeps the incumbent ~20% fee with human recruiters, and the open niches rest on inference |
| Lead gen is last | **Right for the market, wrong for inbound intake** | Consumer lead supply scores worst of any segment on neutral verification. Inbound intake priced per booked appointment scores among the best |
| Recovery audit is a better alternative | **Right, if the segment is retail deductions** | Commercial AP duplicate recovery is a shrinking, self-detectable pool. Amazon FBA was automated away in November 2024. Deductions and freight hold up |
| Property tax appeals are a better alternative | **Right, with a caveat** | It has the cleanest public outcome record of the five. Residential mass filing is the trap, and fees at that end have already fallen from 50% to $49 flat |

## Scoring the five markets on one countability scale

The earlier outcome-pricing report found that outcome pricing works where an event is **cheap to count, attributable, auditable by both sides and low-stakes**, usually inside a contingency market that predates AI. Each researcher scored their market on that lens from 1 to 5, but they did not use the same scale. The table below reconciles them so that **5 is always the most favorable to outcome pricing**. For clawback that means low exposure. For stakes it means low stakes. For "fee holds" it means the fee resists compression by AI, platforms or the paying side.

Three reconciliations mattered. The property-tax notes scored residential stakes as "2 (low stakes, low liability)", which runs against the rest of the scale. Here low stakes are rescored as 4 and commercial as 2. The tax-credit notes scored "AI collapses the fee? Yes, partly (4)", which measures collapse rather than durability. It becomes a 2 here. The recovery-audit notes already scored fee-collapse risk so that 1 means high risk, and those scores carry over. The mean column misleads, and the way it misleads is itself the report's main analytical finding. **Amazon FBA reimbursement has the highest mean of any segment (4.0) and is the worst business in the table.** Freight audit (3.9) is close behind. The same features that make an event cheap for both sides to count also make it cheap for the paying side to detect, prevent or bring in-house. Amazon began proactively reimbursing most FBA warehouse losses on 2024-11-01, cut the claim window from 18 months to 60 days, and from March 2025 valued reimbursements at manufacturing cost ([Forceget](https://forceget.com/blog/amazon-reimbursement-policy-update-2025/); [eComEngine](https://www.ecomengine.com/blog/fba-reimbursement-policy)). Freight audit's volume is real, but the best-funded entrant, Loop, prices as a platform, and much of the market is bundled audit-plus-payment ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/loop/)). The ranking therefore uses "fee holds" and "stakes" as gates. A segment scoring 2 or below on fee durability cannot carry outcome pricing however well it counts. The mean only orders the segments that pass.

Four scores are the biggest judgment calls. Attribution in property tax (2.5) rests on one competitor's analysis of HCAD data, and a year-on-year reversal makes it fragile (see the caution section). "Fee holds" for small commercial property appeals (4) rests on an unsourced assumption that commercial contingency rates have not compressed. The nursing-perm row rests almost entirely on inference, because the notes found no sourced fee percentage or AI entrant in that niche. The retail-deductions fee score (3) assumes that contested items keep their value even though entrants price like software, and Glimpse does not disclose pricing ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/25/a16z-backed-glimpse-raises-new-funds-accelerates-dispute-tracking-automation-for-cpg-brands/)).

## Five wedges, one trap each

### 1. Recovery audit: fight retailer deductions, not supplier duplicates

The wedge is **CPG brands disputing retailer deductions**, priced as a share of dollars recovered on disputed items. The channel is the brand's finance and accounts-receivable team, working through the retailers' own dispute portals. This is where the agent acts for a claimant against a counterparty that will not correct itself. Each deduction is a discrete dispute, the retailer portal is a semi-neutral verifier, and volumes run to thousands of items per brand. Glimpse raised a **$35M Series A led by a16z in March 2026**, bringing its total to about $52M. It serves 200+ brands with human oversight on disputes ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/25/a16z-backed-glimpse-raises-new-funds-accelerates-dispute-tracking-automation-for-cpg-brands/); [Glimpse](https://www.tryglimpse.com/post/seriesa)). Its vendor-reported demo is an agent reviewing 17,000 deductions for a $1B CPG company in under 24 hours ([Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/glimpse-raises-35m-to-automate-retail-deductions-cf3235c8)). Freight and parcel audit is the runner-up. Contracted rate tables make verification clean and volumes are enormous, since **Cass processed $36.45B of transportation spend in 2025** ([Cass 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/708781/000070878126000010/cass-20251231.htm)). But Loop's **$95M Series C** in April 2026 went to a platform, not a contingency agent ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/04/17/supply-chain-ai-startup-loop-secures-95m-investment/)), so outcome pricing there competes with SaaS.

**The trap is the segment the earlier opinion implied by default: generic AP duplicate recovery.** Commercial AP recovery still charges 20–40% contingency ([apexanalytix](https://www.apexanalytix.com/resources/blog/recovery-audit-cost/)). But PRGX says it analyzed $2.3T of spend to find $2.3B, about 0.1% ([PRGX](https://www.prgx.com/guides/ap-recovery-audit-services-guide/)). Ardian took PRGX private for only **$195M** ([Ardian](https://www.ardian.com/press-releases/ardian-and-prgx-announce-acquisition-close)), and prevention tools sit in front of the ERP to stop duplicates before payment ([apexanalytix](https://www.apexanalytix.com/solutions/audit-recovery/overpayment-prevention/)). A second trap sits inside deductions itself: aggressive auto-disputing can get throttled by retailers, so precision matters more than volume. Healthcare payment integrity is a separate game for well-capitalized players. The Medicare RAC fee is set by bid at about 9–12.5% and is returned if a finding is overturned at any appeal level ([FAH](https://fah.org/issues-advocacy/medicare/medicare-recovery-audit-contractor-program/); [CMS](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/fy2023-medicare-and-medicaid-report-congress.pdf)). Payers are moving to pre-claim prevention, as with Codoxo's $35M Series C ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/codoxo-caps-breakout-year-with-oversubscribed-35m-series-c-cementing-significant-market-momentum--leadership-in-pre-claim-payment-integrity-302689012.html)).

### 2. Property tax appeals: represent fewer, bigger cases

Property tax appeals have the cleanest outcome record of the five markets. A government body decides, and the result lands on the certified roll and the tax bill. The fee is mechanical, computed as (original value − final value) × tax rate, and O'Connor bills "for the protested year only" ([O'Connor](https://www.poconnor.com/question/fees-billed-current-future-years/)). Clawback risk is low, and in New Jersey the Freeze Act locks a win for the appeal year plus two more ([LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-18-12A-1-13)). The pool is large: US property tax revenue was **$797B in 2024** ([NAHB, citing Census](https://www.nahb.org/blog/2025/03/state-local-property-tax-collection-2024)).

The recommended wedge is **small and mid-size commercial properties in annual-notice states, starting with Texas**. These are the cases under the threshold where Ryan's rolled-up practice concentrates. They should be handled by a TDLR-registered consulting firm, or by an attorney-led firm where counsel is required, using AI for comps and evidence packets. Pricing is contingency on first-year savings per protested year, at or below prevailing commercial rates. Those rates are reported anecdotally at 20–35% but were not sourced. This report departs here from the property-tax notes, which favored selective Texas residential representation. The residential economics are thin. Ownwell reports average savings of **$774 per customer** at a 25% fee, or roughly $194 per case ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ownwell-raises-50m-launches-national-service-to-streamline-property-tax-appeals-and-make-home-ownership-more-affordable-302692103.html)). AI packet tools already sell DIY filings for **$45–$149 flat** ([ProtestMax.ai](https://protestmax.ai/); [AppealDesk](https://www.appealdesk.com/compare/appealdesk-vs-diy); [taxappeal.app](https://taxappeal.app/)). Agents filed about 90% of Travis County's 205,000 protests ([Austin Bulldog](https://theaustinbulldog.org/205000-property-value-protests-filed/)). Licensing helps the commercial wedge. Texas requires TDLR registration for paid consultants ([TDLR](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ptc/ptcfaq.htm)), New Jersey treats non-attorney contingency appeals as unauthorized practice of law ([NJ ACPE](https://law.justia.com/cases/new-jersey/advisory-committee-on-professional-ethics/1992/cua25-1.html)), and Cook County requires corporations to appear through counsel ([Cook County BOR](https://www.cookcountyboardofreview.com/assessment-appeals/commercial)). Those gates keep $49 packets from reaching representation fees.

**The trap is volume filing.** Harris County agent filings rose from 81,450 in 2023 to **312,358 in 2025**. In 2025 agents won **16%** of protests while self-filers won **71%**, and self-filers also got larger median reductions ($20,640 vs $13,202 of market value) ([Square Deal, HCAD data](https://blog.squaredeal.tax/texas/harris-county-protest-data/)). Assessors are also defending values more actively. The Cook County Assessor built a team to defend large commercial assessments at the BOR ([CCAO](https://www.cookcountyassessoril.gov/news/assessors-office-defends-higher-data-center-assessments-appeal-hearings)).

### 3. Lead generation: price intake, not leads

Buying leads per unit is already the norm. **Angi booked $587.1M of US lead revenue in 2025**, down only 3% while total service requests fell 10% ([Angi 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1705110/000170511026000011/angi-20251231.htm)). Google Local Services Ads charges "for each valid lead" ([Google](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7195435?hl=en)). So buyers do not need to be sold on outcome pricing. The problem is the verifier. In consumer lead supply, the seller or platform judges validity. Google removed manual LSA disputes around July 2024 ([Blue Corona](https://www.bluecorona.com/blog/google-local-service-ads-lead-dispute-deprecation)), and a fraud-detection vendor reports about 25% of lead-gen traffic as fake ([Anura](https://www.anura.io/lead-generation-fraud-costs)). That segment scores 1 on neutral verification. It deserves last place.

**The wedge is inbound intake: answering, qualifying and booking leads the buyer has already paid for.** It is priced per booked appointment or signed case, and the record is the buyer's own call logs and CRM. The first targets are personal-injury firms, whose leads cost $200–800+, and home-services contractors buying LSA or Angi leads ([leadgen-economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/insurance-mortgage-solar-legal-leads/)). The value is mostly recovering leakage: a cited Clio study found only 40% of law firms answered calls, though it was not verified at the source ([Technology.org](https://www.technology.org/2026/04/10/best-ai-virtual-receptionists-for-a-law-firm-managing-intake-calls-from-prospective-clients-in-2026/)). The category is thinly funded, as Caseflood's $3.2M seed shows ([Artificial Lawyer](https://www.artificiallawyer.com/2025/07/30/y-combinator-backed-caseflood-bags-3-2m-for-voice-agents/)). The ceiling on the fee is the $500-a-month AI receptionist sold as SaaS, so the per-outcome price must sit well below the buyer's lead cost. Outbound B2B "per meeting held" is a viable second niche. Agencies already sell it at $300–600 per qualified meeting ([Salesar](https://salesar.io/blog/b2b-appointment-setting-costs-in-2025-pricing-models-explained)). But cold-email reply rates have fallen to about 3.43% ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)), and 11x shows how "booked" differs from "held". TechCrunch sources alleged 70–80% churn and customer logos the company did not have ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/24/a16z-and-benchmark-backed-11x-has-been-claiming-customers-it-doesnt-have)).

**The trap is outbound consumer AI voice.** The FCC ruled in 2024 that AI voices are "artificial" under the TCPA, so these calls need prior express consent ([FCC 24-17](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf)). Damages run $500–1,500 per call with no cap, and state mini-TCPAs add limits on time of day and message frequency ([leadgen-economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/state-mini-tcpa-laws-ftsa-otsa/)). The one-to-one consent rule was vacated and repealed ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca11/24-10277/24-10277-2025-01-24.html); [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/blogs/fcc-repeals-one-one-consent-rule-following-eleventh-circuit-decision)). That keeps shared-lead selling legal, but it does not remove per-call exposure for an agent that dials at scale.

### 4. Recruitment: the model works, and a marketplace already owns it

Recruitment has the best neutral verifier of the five. A start date and payroll record are binary, third-party events, and the ~20% contingency fee with a 60–90 day guarantee is a norm every buyer understands ([Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/negotiate-recruiter-fees/)). The evidence also confirms that outcome pricing works here in the AI era. **Paraform raised a $40M Series B in March 2026**, serves 1,000+ companies at an average placed salary of $260k, and has paid recruiters over $50M ([Paraform](https://www.paraform.com/blog/series-b)). Aggregators report it claims more than $100M annualized ([Second Talent](https://www.secondtalent.com/resources/top-ai-recruiting-startups-usa/)). But Paraform charges the incumbent rate and uses human recruiters. The largest "AI recruiting" revenue, at Mercor and micro1, is contractor data labor for AI labs rather than permanent placements. **91% of Mercor's H1 2026 revenue came from foundation-model companies** ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/richardnieva/2026/07/09/mercor-fundraise/); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/mercor/)). Pure-software tools price per seat. Juicebox charges $99–199 per seat, and LinkedIn Hiring Assistant is listed at £6,350 per licence per year ([G-Cloud](https://assets.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud-14/documents/712281/850052488849651-pricing-document-2025-10-20-1430.pdf)).

The recommended wedge is **permanent placement in nursing, allied health and licensed trades**. It runs as a marketplace in which human recruiters make the submission decision, while AI does the sourcing and credential checks against state license databases. Pricing is a flat fee per start with a guarantee, set against the hospital's alternative. A 13-week travel med-surg contract costs about $54–72k ([Sigma Nursing](https://jobs.sigmanursing.org/career-resources/employer-8/travel-nurse-agencies-vs-direct-hire-2026-124)). This is the weakest-evidenced wedge in the report. The notes found no sourced perm fee percentage and no AI entrant in the niche. Illinois bans temp-to-perm conversion fees for nurse agencies and Minnesota caps agency charges, per the same aggregator source, and the statutes need checking. The application flood strengthens the case for any outcome-priced sourcer that can prove a candidate is real. Greenhouse reports **254 applicants per posting** and a 412% rise in applications per recruiter since 2022 ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/07/60693446/job-seekers-are-using-ai-to-apply-everywhere-recruiters-are-using-ai-to-filter-them-greenhouse-ceo-calls-it-a-doom-loop)).

**The trap is letting the agent rank candidates on its own.** In *Mobley v. Workday*, the court certified a nationwide ADEA collective against a vendor, and in June 2026 it declined to dismiss the California FEHA claims ([Clearinghouse](https://clearinghouse.net/case/44074/); [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.insidetechlaw.com/blog/2026/06/behind-the-privilege-shield-safeguarding-ai-bias-testing-data-in-employment-decisions)). New York's comptroller found LL144 enforcement "ineffective", and enforcement is now tightening ([NY OSC](https://www.osc.ny.gov/state-agencies/audits/2025/12/02/enforcement-local-law-144-automated-employment-decision-tools)). EU high-risk obligations now apply from 2 December 2027 ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)). Attribution is the second trap. When a candidate is already in the ATS or was also submitted by another agency, the fee is disputed, so timestamped submissions are mandatory.

### 5. Tax credits: sell the evidence file, not a share of the refund

The R&D credit is a large pool. JCT puts it at **$188.9B over FY2025–2029**, about $38B a year ([CRS R48848](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R48848.html)). But most of the value goes to large companies: in 2013, firms with $250M+ in receipts were 14% of claimants and took 85% of credit value. Those firms use Big 4 firms on non-contingent terms. The contingency segment is SMB and mid-market, where tickets are small. Fondo's YC launch cited an average startup credit of $21,000 ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/H3v-fondo-automatically-get-cash-back-from-the-irs-average-startup-gets-21-000)), which yields a fee of about $3–4K at 15–20%. The startup payroll-offset segment is already a feature inside Gusto, Pilot and Warp/Fondo. MainStreet, which took a cut of credits found, cut 30% of staff in 2022 and went to Employer.com in May 2025 with a 15-person team ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3003322)).

Verification is the core problem. The IRS is a neutral verifier, but accepting a return is not validating it. The real verdict comes at audit or when the statute closes, about three years later. Since October 2021, a refund claim must identify every business component, the research activities in each, who performed them and what they sought to discover ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/required-information-for-a-valid-research-credit-claim-for-refund)). Form 6765 Section G makes business-component detail mandatory for tax years after 2025. Qualified small businesses making the payroll election are exempt, as are filers with QREs of $1.5M or less and gross receipts of $50M or less ([IRS i6765](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i6765)). Outside the US, HMRC's crackdown shows where this leads. UK claimant companies roughly halved from about 86,000 (2021) to about 44,000 (2024), and **77% of checked claims were adjusted** ([ForrestBrown](https://forrestbrown.co.uk/news/hmrc-annual-report-r-and-d-tax-analysis/)).

The wedge follows from these rules. **An agent reads engineering systems (git, tickets, HRIS, cloud costs) and produces the Section G evidence file for mid-market filers above the exemption thresholds.** It is priced as a flat fee per claim or per business component, with an audit-defense warranty or fee holdback until the statute closes. It is sold through CPA firms and embedded in payroll and ledger platforms, the way Neo.Tax is embedded in Gusto ([Gusto](https://gusto.com/product/integrations/neo_tax)). CodeROI already pitches Section G tooling built from code repositories ([CodeROI](https://www.coderoi.com/section-g)). If the Circular 230 proposal is finalized, credentialed firms would lose contingency pricing on original and amended returns ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/12/26/2024-29371/regulations-governing-practice-before-the-internal-revenue-service)). Flat-priced software would then become the natural supplier. No final rule had been found as of 2026-09-30 ([Federal Tax Updates](https://federaltaxupdates.com/50)). Adjacent incentives rank differently. Cost segregation has a tailwind from OBBBA's permanent 100% bonus depreciation ([Cherry Bekaert](https://www.cbh.com/insights/articles/trumps-tax-bill-bonus-depreciation-cost-segregation/)). 179D ends for construction beginning after 2026-06-30 ([CTI](https://ctillc.com/blog/cost-seg-179d-itc-stacking-strategy/)). WOTC lapsed on 2025-12-31 ([CRS R43729](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R43729/R43729.9.pdf)).

**The trap is pricing as a percentage of the credit.** It invites the ERC framing, in which a fee based on the refund helps define a promoter who faces a $1,000 per-failure due-diligence penalty ([RSM](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2025/what-you-need-to-know-about-obbba-and-the-employee-retention-tax-credit.html)). It also rewards overclaiming in a channel the IRS polices hardest: §174A retroactivity makes 2022–2024 amended returns attractive precisely where refund claims are scrutinized ([Plante Moran](https://www.plantemoran.com/explore-our-thinking/insight/2025/07/obbb-restores-expensing-of-domestic-section-174-re-costs)). The ERC ended in a processing moratorium, 28,000 disallowance letters, 460 criminal cases and repayment rates of 80–85% under voluntary disclosure ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/coronavirus/frequently-asked-questions-about-the-employee-retention-credit); [EisnerAmper](https://www.eisneramper.com/insights/tax/irs-provides-updates-on-efforts-to-combat-erc-fraud-via-various-programs/); [Drake](https://www.drakesoftware.com/taxing-subjects/erc-fraud-victims-voluntary-disclosure-program/)).

## Five lessons that hold across all five markets

**Volume filing collapses win rates, because the verifier triages mass filers.** Harris County is the sharpest data point. The overall protest success rate fell from 84% in 2021 to 29% in 2023–2025, and agent filings nearly quadrupled over two years while the agent win rate fell to 16% ([Square Deal](https://blog.squaredeal.tax/texas/harris-county-protest-data/)). The same pattern shows up elsewhere. HMRC adjusted 77% of the UK R&D claims it checked, the IRS froze ERC processing, and Google replaced buyer disputes with automated credits. Wherever an adjudicator faces a flood of agent-generated claims, it lowers the prior on every claim. An outcome-priced agent is paid on wins, not filings, so its edge is case selection. Filing throughput works against it. Medicare RAC contracts make this explicit: overturn rates must stay below 10% at first-level appeal, and the fee is returned on any overturn ([Cotiviti](https://www.cotiviti.com/hubfs/Cotiviti%20-%20CMS%20RAC%20PDFs/CMS%20RAC%20Provider%20Overview.pdf)).

**Fees collapse where the paying side can detect the error itself.** Amazon's auto-reimbursement, ERP prevention tools and payers moving to pre-claim review all shrink the contingency base by design. The durable pools involve a claimant against a counterparty that will not self-correct: a brand against a retailer's deduction, a shipper against a carrier, an owner against an appraisal district. This amends the earlier countability framework. An event must be cheap for both sides to count, but **not so cheap that the payer automates the count**. The best segment sits between countable and self-detectable.

**Regulators define bad actors by the pricing unit.** OBBBA's ERC promoter category turns partly on a refund-based fee, and the proposed Circular 230 rule would make contingent fees on returns "disreputable conduct." Where the counterparty is the Treasury, a percentage of the refund is itself a red flag. Property tax is the counter-example. Texas licenses consultants and explicitly allows contingency, flat, portfolio or blended pricing ([TDLR](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ptc/ptcfaq.htm)). Contingency is therefore not suspect in general. It is suspect where the fee payer's liability outlives the fee.

**The winners are marketplaces, licensed firms or platform features with AI inside, not pure agents.** Paraform keeps human recruiters on the submit decision. Ownwell is a licensed appeals firm, and it now also ships an AI packet for states it does not serve. Glimpse keeps humans on disputes. The surviving R&D credit products are features inside Gusto and Pilot. Wherever a software-only agent appears, it prices per seat, per contact or per packet: Juicebox, AiSDR ([AiSDR](https://aisdr.com/pricing/)), and the $49 property-tax tools. The reason is structural. Licensing (TDLR, UPL, counsel rules), liability (*Mobley*, TCPA) and hearing appearances all need a legal person that owns the outcome.

**Roll-ups, not displacement, have produced the exits.** Machinify, backed by New Mountain, bought Performant for about **$670M**, closing in October 2025. That gave an AI payment-integrity platform the two Medicare RAC regions Performant still holds ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251021569768/en/Machinify-Completes-Acquisition-of-Performant-Healthcare-Accelerating-Intelligent-Healthcare-Payments); [CMS](https://www.cms.gov/data-research/monitoring-programs/medicare-fee-service-compliance-programs/medicare-fee-service-recovery-audit-program)). Ryan bought Marvin F. Poer, "the second-largest property tax consulting firm in the US" (2022), then RETC (2023) and Popp Hutcheson (2024) ([Ryan](https://ryan.com/about-ryan/press-room/marvin-f-poer-and-company-acquisition/); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240306035672/en/Ryan-Strengthens-Property-Tax-Practice-with-Acquisition-of-Consulting-Business-of-Popp-Hutcheson-PLLC)). SPS Commerce absorbed Carbon6's Amazon reimbursement service ([Hack'celeration](https://hackceleration.com/labs/review/carbon6)). Workday paid about $1B for Paradox ([Workday](https://newsroom.workday.com/2025-10-01-Workday-Completes-Acquisition-of-Paradox)). MainStreet shows the failed version: a contingency startup absorbed as a small acqui-hire. Legacy contingency firms are cheap relative to the spend they audit, as PRGX's $195M price shows. For an AI entrant, buying a licence holder or contract holder can be faster than displacing one.

## Figures to treat with caution

Several figures behind this report are aggregator-grade, vendor-sourced or in conflict. Each one is flagged here rather than resolved silently.

- **Cook County appeal success rates conflict.** WTTW reports that just over 56% of appeals received a reduction ([WTTW](https://news.wttw.com/2025/11/25/cook-county-board-review-reopen-2025-property-tax-appeals-window)). A law-firm blog reports 39.4% for 2024 and 28.4% for 2022 ([cookcountytaxappeal.com](https://www.cookcountytaxappeal.com/property-tax-appeal-blogs/property-tax-appeal-results-savings-reduction-rates-2025)). The gap is probably a matter of denominator or year. The record figure of 290,533 appeals comes from a search-snippet summary, not the BOR table.
- **"$32B of R&D credits in tax year 2021"** surfaced in a search summary that could not be traced to any SOI table. It is excluded. The last official SOI total is **$12.6B for TY2014** ([IRS SOI](https://www.irs.gov/statistics/soi-tax-stats-corporation-research-credit)). The ~$38B a year figure is JCT's cost estimate, not a count of claims.
- **Harris County's 16% vs 71% split** comes from a competing tax-protest firm's analysis of HCAD data. In 2024 the pattern was reversed (agents 27%, DIY 23%). A one-year swing that large suggests a change in mix or definition, so the figure needs checking against raw ARB data before it anchors a business plan.
- **Fee-pool estimates** for tax credits (~$0.6–2.3B), property tax (high hundreds of millions to ~$2B) and US perm recruiting (~$1–1.25M placements) are researchers' back-of-envelope arithmetic. No sourced market size exists for R&D consulting or recovery audit.
- **US permanent placement at ~$19.7B (11% of staffing)** is an aggregator split ([Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/state-of-recruitment-agencies-report/)). It conflicts with ASA's $113.5B temp figure ([StaffingPulse](https://www.thestaffingpulse.com/news/revenue-decline-2025.html)).
- **Revenue claims:** Paraform's >$100M annualized comes via aggregators. Commentators put Mercor's "$2B" gross closer to ~$600M net ([ValueAddVC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/how-does-mercor-make-money-2b-arr-20b-valuation-and-the-expert-data-marketplace-explained)). Ownwell's 86% success rate and $774 average saving, and O'Connor's $213M saved, are unaudited company claims. Loop's ~$44M ARR is a Sacra estimate.
- **Lead-gen figures** are mostly aggregator- or vendor-grade: the insurance and mortgage market sizes, the 25% fraud rate (from a vendor that sells fraud detection), the $80–150 AI-SDR cost per meeting (vendor-authored), and speed-to-lead lift, which rests on a study from about 2007.
- **Duplicate-payment rates conflict.** They are given as 0.8–2.0% of disbursements by count (APQC, via an aggregator), "up to 1.5% of cash" and 0.1–0.5% of invoices (IOFM, via aggregators) ([Transparent](https://transparentglobal.com/blog/what-percentage-of-ap-spend-is-lost-to-duplicate-payments-industry-benchmarks/); [ExpensePoint](https://www.expensepoint.com/blog/duplicate-payments/)).
- **Dates and terms are unverified.** Boast's $23M Series A is dated about 2021 by its release ID and May 2025 by a search summary. The ERC assessment-statute extension is six years per RSM, but a search summary says it runs to 2027-04-15. Carbon6's sale to SPS is dated only by a review site. Medicare RAC overpayments of ~$352.5M (FY2023) and NYC's 57,302 appeal applications come from search snippets of PDFs that could not be parsed.

## Conclusion

The countability framework survives this test but needs a second axis. Countability tells an agent builder which unit to bill. **Counterparty resistance tells them whether the fee will still exist in three years.** The five markets sort cleanly on that second axis. Fees are collapsing where the payer can see the error (Amazon, ERP duplicates) or where the regulator treats the fee itself as suspect (the Treasury in tax credits). They hold where an adjudicator or adversary must be persuaded case by case: retailers, appraisal districts, hiring managers. The earlier opinion chose markets by the size of the pool and the familiarity of contingency pricing. The evidence chooses them by who controls the verdict and whether that party wants the agent to win.

The practical upshot for a builder is that the product is rarely "an agent that charges a percentage." It is a licensed firm, marketplace or embedded feature whose AI lowers its cost per win, and which files fewer, stronger claims than its competitors. Where fees are compressing anyway, as in tax credits and residential appeals, the winning move is to price the work product flat and let the volume filers absorb the falling win rates. Two developments would change these rankings. One is a final Circular 230 rule, which would hand the mid-market credit business to flat-priced software overnight. The other is any disclosed retailer-deduction win rate or fee, which would confirm or break the case for the top-ranked market.
