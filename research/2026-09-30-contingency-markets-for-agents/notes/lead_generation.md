# Pay-per-lead / pay-per-appointment lead generation as a market for outcome-priced AI agents

Research date: 2026-09-30. Labels: **[confirmed]** = primary source (filing, regulator, court, vendor's own page); **[reported]** = journalism; **[aggregator]** = vendor blog, SEO benchmark page or compiled stats of unclear provenance. Treat aggregator figures as directional only. About 20 tool calls were made, so coverage is broad but shallow in places (see Gaps).

## 1. Market size and structure

### Takeaway
Buying leads per unit is already the normal way to buy in home services, insurance, mortgage, legal and solar. Angi alone booked about $587M in US per-lead revenue in 2025, and insurance and mortgage lead markets are each reported at about $5–7B in yearly transaction value. So pricing per outcome is not a new idea that AI agents need to sell. The per-unit price anchors are already set, and they are high: about $15–150 per insurance or mortgage lead, $200–800+ per personal-injury lead, and $300–600 per B2B meeting held.

### Cited Findings
- **Angi FY2025 [confirmed]:** Revenue was $1,030.5M, down 13% from $1,185.1M in 2024. US Lead revenue was $587.1M (−3%), Advertising $214.9M (−31%), Services $72.4M (−23%) and Membership $29.4M (−32%). Proprietary service requests were 13.9M (+4%), Network service requests 1.64M (−58%) and total service requests 15.5M (−10%). — [Angi 10-K FY2025, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1705110/000170511026000011/angi-20251231.htm)
- **Thumbtack [reported]:** FY2024 revenue was about $400M (+27% YoY), with about 300,000 small businesses booking work through the platform. The company is private and I found no 2025 figure. — [Fast Company, 2025](https://www.fastcompany.com/91311830/home-services-company-thumbtack-is-thriving-even-as-the-real-estate-market-stays-slow)
- **Google Local Services Ads pricing model [confirmed]:** "You're charged for each valid lead you receive through your Local Services ad." Price varies by location, job type and bidding mode, and ads stop once the monthly budget is reached. — [Google LSA Help, "How leads work", accessed 2026-09-30](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7195435?hl=en)
- **Total US ad spend (context) [reported/forecast]:** eMarketer's best case was US media ad spend up 6.7% in 2025 to $422B. I found no public eMarketer figure for spend on lead generation specifically. — [eMarketer, US Ad Spending 2025](https://www.emarketer.com/content/us-ad-spending-2025)
- **Insurance lead market [aggregator]:** about $5.2–6.8B in yearly transaction value. The same source says MediaAlpha's 2024 revenue was $865M (+123%) on $1.5B of transaction value. Verify against MediaAlpha's 10-K before using. — [leadgen-economy.com, early 2026](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/insurance-mortgage-solar-legal-leads/)
- **Mortgage lead market [aggregator]:** about $5.6B in yearly transaction value. — [same](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/insurance-mortgage-solar-legal-leads/)
- **Legal advertising [aggregator]:** more than $2.5B in 2024 (+14% YoY), with 26.9M local legal-service ads aired. — [same](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/insurance-mortgage-solar-legal-leads/)

  - By target seniority: SMB meetings run $50–300, mid-market $300–600 and C-suite $600–1,500+. — [Outbound Pros, 2026](https://outboundpros.io/blog/b2b-appointment-setting-pricing-2026)

### Inferences
- Pay-per-lead is an established, liquid market. An AI agent does not have to convince buyers to pay per outcome. It has to compete on cost of supply against incumbents (Angi, Google LSA, MediaAlpha, QuinStreet-type networks), and those incumbents own the demand side: search traffic and consumer intent.
- Angi's per-lead line is holding up (−3%) while total service requests fell 10%. That points to rising revenue per lead: the per-unit price is not collapsing at the aggregator level, at least not yet.
- The B2B "pay per meeting held" agency market is the clearest ready-made business model for an outcome-priced AI SDR, at $150–600 per meeting.

### Gaps
- I found no credible, dated total for US lead-generation spend in 2025–2026. eMarketer data is paywalled, and I did not locate LeadsCouncil figures.
- Thumbtack 2025 revenue and per-lead prices were not found.
- Google LSA average cost per lead by trade was not found from a primary source.
- Education and healthcare CPLs were not sourced.
- MediaAlpha numbers are secondhand and need checking against its 10-K.

## 2. AI entrants and outcomes

### Takeaway
AI SDRs mostly charge per seat, per contact or per action, not per meeting. The per-meeting figures ($80–150 per booked meeting) are effective costs that third parties calculated, not contract terms. The most-funded company in the category, 11x, was reported in March 2025 to have churn of 70–80% and to list customers it did not have. On the inbound side, AI voice agents that answer every call have a clean value case (most calls to law firms go unanswered) but are early and thinly funded.

### Cited Findings
- **11x [reported]:** TechCrunch (2025-03-24), drawing on about two dozen sources, reported that 11x:
  - listed companies as customers that denied being customers. ZoomInfo said it had only a one-month trial, was "not a customer" and threatened legal action.
  - counted contracts that customers had exited through three-month break clauses in its headline revenue.
  - had churn of 70–80% according to sources. 11x disputed this and cited 79% retention.
  
  Sources also described the product not working as expected and hallucinating. — [TechCrunch, 2025-03-24](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/24/a16z-and-benchmark-backed-11x-has-been-claiming-customers-it-doesnt-have)
- **AiSDR [confirmed, vendor page]:** Plans cost $250/month (Solo, 200 contacts), $900 (Explore, 800 contacts) and $2,500 (Scale, 2,500 contacts), with no seat cap. Explore and Scale are billed quarterly in advance. The pricing is per contact volume, not per meeting. — [AiSDR pricing, accessed 2026-09-30](https://aisdr.com/pricing/)
- **Artisan [aggregator]:** Usage-based pricing, sales-led quotes, typically $1–2.5K/month. — [Artisan blog](https://www.artisan.co/blog/how-much-does-an-ai-sdr-cost-pricing-compared-to-human-sdrs); [formanorden.com, 2026](https://formanorden.com/blog/ai-sdr-pricing/)
- **Salesforce Agentforce [aggregator]:** Salesforce moved from about $2 per conversation to about $0.10 per action (Flex Credits). This prices per action, not per outcome. — [Cleanlist AI SDR Pricing Index, 2026-07-23](https://www.cleanlist.ai/blog/2026-07-23-ai-sdr-pricing-statistics)
- **Effective cost per meeting [aggregator, vendor-authored]:**
  - AI SDRs work out to about $80–150 per booked meeting, against about $533 for a human SDR.
  - AiSDR Solo is estimated at about $125 per meeting and Artisan mid-range at about $88.
  
  These estimates come from vendor or affiliate blogs, so there is a conflict of interest. — [Cleanlist](https://www.cleanlist.ai/blog/2026-07-23-ai-sdr-pricing-statistics); [Artisan blog](https://www.artisan.co/blog/how-much-does-an-ai-sdr-cost-pricing-compared-to-human-sdrs)
- **Law-firm inbound [reported]:**
  - Caseflood.ai (YC) raised $3.2M in July 2025 for AI voice intake agents for law firms. — [Artificial Lawyer, 2025-07-30](https://www.artificiallawyer.com/2025/07/30/y-combinator-backed-caseflood-bags-3-2m-for-voice-agents/)
  - A Clio 2024 study is cited as finding that only 40% of law firms answered phone calls, down from 56% in 2019. I did not verify it at Clio. — [Technology.org summary, 2026-04-10](https://www.technology.org/2026/04/10/best-ai-virtual-receptionists-for-a-law-firm-managing-intake-calls-from-prospective-clients-in-2026/)
- **Home services / local businesses [reported]:** Scorpion launched "Scorpion Convert", an AI voice and messaging intake and scheduling tool, in October 2025. — [Wikipedia: Scorpion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorpion_(company))
- **Speed-to-lead evidence:**
  - The foundational study is Oldroyd's MIT/InsideSales Lead Response Management study (about 2007, 15,000+ leads). It found that responding within 5 minutes versus 30 minutes made contact about 100x more likely and qualification about 21x more likely. The study is old and I found no replication from 2024–2026. — [Verse.ai summary](https://verse.ai/blog/speed-to-lead-statistics); [digitalapplied 2026](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/speed-to-lead-response-time-benchmarks-2026-data-playbook)
  - The Velocify figure is widely repeated: leads contacted within 1 minute convert 391% better than at 5 minutes. It is also old (2010s). — [leadgen-economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/insurance-mortgage-solar-legal-leads/)
  - Also widely repeated but unverified: "78% of B2B buyers buy from the first responder" and "average follow-up is 47 hours". — [Amplemarket](https://www.amplemarket.com/blog/how-to-win-deals-faster-speed-to-lead-statistics-you-need-to-know)

### Inferences
- The outbound AI SDR category has a verification problem, which 11x illustrates. Buyers struggled to tell real meetings from booked noise, and they left through break clauses. Charging per qualified meeting held would answer that problem directly. That no major AI SDR does so is a notable gap in the market, or a signal that unit economics per meeting are unattractive with today's reply rates.
- Inbound speed-to-lead is the easiest wedge to verify. Leads are already paid for (through LSA, Angi or ads), response time is objectively measurable, and the lift from answering at all is large. Most of the value is simply picking up the calls that currently go unanswered, so this is a lower-risk place to price on outcomes, for example per booked appointment or per signed case.
- The speed-to-lead evidence base is mostly one study from about 2007 plus vendor repetitions. Diligence should ask for A/B data from the buyer's own funnel.

### Gaps
- No public churn or retention data for Artisan, Regie, AiSDR or Qualified Piper.
- Pricing for Qualified Piper and Regie.ai was not retrieved.
- I found no example of a major AI SDR billing per meeting held. Smaller "AI-powered agencies" may, but I did not verify any.
- 11x's status after the scandal (leadership, revenue) as of 2026 was not researched.
- No independent 2024–2026 speed-to-lead RCT or dataset.

## 3. Disputes, fraud and attribution

### Takeaway
About a quarter of lead-gen traffic is reported as fraudulent. Platforms have moved dispute handling from buyer-initiated to platform-decided: Google removed manual LSA disputes in 2024. In today's markets, the seller or platform is also the verifier. That conflict of interest is the core weakness an AI agent's outcome pricing must solve.

### Cited Findings
- **Fraud rate [aggregator, vendor-sourced]:**
  - Anura's report says 25% of all lead-gen traffic is fake (bots, click farms, fraud rings), with rates commonly 25–28% across advertisers in 2025. Anura sells fraud detection, so it has a commercial interest in high figures. — [Anura Lead Generation Fraud Report](https://www.anura.io/lead-generation-fraud-costs); [Heyflow summary](https://heyflow.com/blog/global-ad-fraud-report/)
  - The TrustedForm-era discussion of bot leads at 25% and fabricated consent is also relevant. — [leadgen-economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/bot-leads-25-percent-trustedform-detection-roi/)
- **Google LSA credits [confirmed]:**
  - Google "automatically reassesses charged leads over time and may issue credits for low-quality ones." Advertisers can report poor-quality leads through a feedback survey, and Google "may also occasionally credit" those leads.
  - Automatic credits apply only in the US and Canada, and not for healthcare, tax specialists or EMEA.
  - Leads that are not credited include contact outside business hours, advice-seekers, cancelled bookings, price shoppers, non-response to callbacks and service types not offered. — [Google LSA Help, accessed 2026-09-30](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7195435?hl=en)
- **LSA dispute removal [aggregator/agency]:**
  - The manual dispute button was removed around July 2024 and replaced by automated review of charged leads, reportedly within 72 hours.
  - Credits for "job type not serviced" and "geo not serviced" were ended.
  - Advertisers reported lower lead quality and no recourse through 2025. — [Coalmarch](https://www.coalmarch.com/resources/blog/google-lsa-automated-credits-verified-badge-updates); [Blue Corona](https://www.bluecorona.com/blog/google-local-service-ads-lead-dispute-deprecation); [Footbridge Media](https://www.footbridgemedia.com/marketing-tips/5-changes-google-local-service-ads-contractors-should-know)

### Inferences
- Returns and clawbacks exist but are controlled by the seller or platform, not by a neutral party. Buyers already distrust this. An AI agent priced on outcomes will meet the same fight unless it uses a third-party verification record: TrustedForm or Jornaya consent certificates, call recordings, and CRM stage timestamps.
- Generative AI makes lead fraud cheaper, with synthetic form fills and voice bots. That raises the value of verification and consent-proof infrastructure, which could be an adjacent opportunity.

### Gaps
- Angi's credit and return policy terms were not retrieved from a primary source.
- Thumbtack's refund policy was not retrieved.
- No industry-wide lead return-rate statistics (for example, typical 5–15% return allowances in insurance lead contracts) could be sourced.
- No LeadsCouncil data found.

## 4. Regulatory and deliverability risk

### Takeaway
Two things drive the risk:
- **Outbound AI voice to consumers** needs prior express consent, with $500–1,500 in damages per call and no cap. State mini-TCPAs are driving a wave of lawsuits.
- **Cold email** is squeezed by the Gmail and Yahoo spam-rate ceilings. Reply rates are in long decline, reportedly about 3.4% in 2026.

The regime was loosened in one place: the one-to-one consent rule was vacated and then repealed, which preserved the shared-consent lead-selling model.

### Cited Findings
- **FCC AI-voice ruling [confirmed]:** On 2024-02-08 the FCC unanimously issued Declaratory Ruling FCC 24-17. It holds that AI-generated voices are "artificial" under the TCPA, so these calls need prior express consent and must follow identification, disclosure and opt-out rules. — [FCC 24-17](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf); [FCC news release](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-confirms-tcpa-applies-ai-technologies-generate-human-voices); [Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/fcc-rules-ai-generated-voices-are-artificial-under-the-tcpa.html)
- **Statutory damages [confirmed by statute; summary aggregator]:** $500 per violation, and up to $1,500 if willful or knowing, with no cap. — [Henson Legal, 2026](https://www.henson-legal.com/ai-voice-compliance)
- **One-to-one consent rule [confirmed]:**
  - The Eleventh Circuit unanimously vacated the rule on 2025-01-24 in *Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC*, No. 24-10277, one business day before its 2025-01-27 effective date. The court held that the FCC exceeded its statutory authority, because "prior express consent" does not forbid multi-party consent. — [Justia opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca11/24-10277/24-10277-2025-01-24.html); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/eleventh-circuit-vacates-tcpa-11-consent-rule)
  - The FCC formally repealed the rule on 2025-07-22 in its "Delete, Delete, Delete" proceeding, restoring the earlier consent rules. — [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/blogs/fcc-repeals-one-one-consent-rule-following-eleventh-circuit-decision)
  - Consumer groups (NCLC) sought en banc rehearing. — [NCLC](https://www.nclc.org/consumer-privacy-groups-request-full-court-rehearing-in-11th-circuit-decision-to-overturn-telemarketing-rule/)
- **State mini-TCPAs [aggregator]:**
  - At least 15 states had their own telemarketing statutes by late 2025. Florida's FTSA (2021, amended 2023), Oklahoma, Washington and Maryland are the key ones.
  - Florida, Maryland and Oklahoma presume that a consumer with an in-state area code is located in that state.
  - Florida, Maryland, New Jersey and Oklahoma limit messages to 3 per recipient per 24 hours. Florida and Oklahoma ban commercial texts before 8am and after 8pm.
  - Florida and Oklahoma give a private right of action at $500 per violation, trebled if willful.
  - One South Florida firm reportedly filed more than 100 TCPA suits in March 2025 over time-of-day texting. TCPA filings were reported up about 27% in early 2026. — [leadgen-economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/state-mini-tcpa-laws-ftsa-otsa/); [Enzo tracker](https://enzodialer.com/resources/compliance/state-mini-tcpa-laws)
- **Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules [confirmed via multiple compliance summaries]:**
  - The rules were announced in October 2023 and phased in from February 2024.
  - Senders of 5,000 or more messages per day to Gmail are permanently classified as bulk senders. They must pass SPF and DKIM, publish DMARC and offer one-click unsubscribe.
  - The spam-complaint rate must stay below 0.3%, and Google recommends below 0.1%.
  - Google moved to stricter enforcement (temporary and permanent rejections) in November 2025. — [Red Sift 2026 guide](https://redsift.com/guides/bulk-email-sender-requirements); [MarTech](https://martech.org/new-rules-for-bulk-email-senders-from-google-yahoo-what-you-need-to-know/); [Security Boulevard, 2025-11](https://securityboulevard.com/2025/11/google-and-yahoo-updated-email-authentication-requirements-for-2025/)
  - Microsoft (Outlook) added similar rules in 2025. — [Red Sift](https://redsift.com/guides/bulk-email-sender-requirements)
- **Cold email reply rates [aggregator, vendor data]:**
  - Instantly reports an average reply rate of 3.43% in 2026 (top quartile 5.5%, elite above 10%). It gives a trajectory of 8.5% (2019) to about 5% (2025) to 3.43% (2026), and attributes the decline partly to inbox saturation and low-effort AI-generated outreach. — [Instantly Cold Email Benchmark Report 2026](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026); [Instantly benchmarks](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks/)
  - Other compilations put 2024–2025 B2B averages at 3–5.1%. — [The Digital Bloom](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/cold-outbound-reply-rate-benchmarks/); [Belkins 2026](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates)

### Inferences
- The one-to-one repeal keeps the "one form, many buyers" consumer lead model legal at the federal level. However, AI voice callbacks to those leads still need consent that covers artificial-voice calls. State timing and frequency caps create strict liability exposure per message, and that exposure grows with AI volume.
- Inbound response carries much lower TCPA risk than outbound: the consumer initiated contact, though texts and callbacks still need consent. That is another reason inbound speed-to-lead is the safer form.
- Outbound B2B email faces a volume trap. AI makes sending cheap, reply rates fall, and spam-rate ceilings cap how much any one domain can send. The scarce unit, a real meeting held, is getting more expensive to produce, which supports per-meeting prices rather than collapsing them.

### Gaps
- CAN-SPAM and GDPR/ePrivacy rules for EU B2B cold outreach were not researched in this pass. The known baseline, not re-verified here: CAN-SPAM is opt-out and allows B2B cold email with identification and unsubscribe; in the EU, GDPR legitimate interest plus national ePrivacy rules restrict B2B cold email in some states, for example Germany.
- No primary Google or Yahoo document was fetched.
- No independent (non-vendor) reply-rate time series.
- Oklahoma's 2025 amendments to the OTSA were not checked.

## 5. Verification profile (scored 1–5; 5 = most favorable to outcome-priced AI agents)

### Takeaway
Lead gen scores moderately well on how often outcomes occur and how observable they are, but poorly on neutral verification. The buyer (or the platform) judges quality and has every incentive to reject. The most defensible outcome units are "appointment held" and "case signed" or "job booked", not a raw lead. Inbound speed-to-lead verifies best, because the agent works inside the buyer's own funnel on leads the buyer already paid for.

### Cited Findings (evidence used in scoring)
- Platform-controlled credits and removed disputes (Google LSA, 2024). — [Google LSA Help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7195435?hl=en); [Blue Corona](https://www.bluecorona.com/blog/google-local-service-ads-lead-dispute-deprecation)
- 11x churn and customer-claim allegations: buyers could not verify value and left. — [TechCrunch 2025-03-24](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/24/a16z-and-benchmark-backed-11x-has-been-claiming-customers-it-doesnt-have)
- Fraud reported at 25–28% of traffic. — [Anura](https://www.anura.io/lead-generation-fraud-costs)
- Per-meeting agency pricing at $300–600 is already the norm. — [Salesar](https://salesar.io/blog/b2b-appointment-setting-costs-in-2025-pricing-models-explained)
- Reply rates are falling: 3.43% in 2026. — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)

### Inferences (scores)

| Feature | Outbound B2B (per meeting held) | Inbound speed-to-lead (per booked appt / signed case) | Vertical consumer lead gen (per lead) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Neutral verifier** | 2. The buyer confirms "held" and "qualified", so there is a conflict of interest, but calendar and recording evidence exists. | 3. Events are in the buyer's own CRM and phone logs. Fairly objective ("booked", "showed"), but the buyer still controls the data. | 1. The platform or seller judges validity (LSA automated credits). Buyers dispute. Fraud is about 25%. |
| **Feedback latency** | 3. Meeting held within days to weeks. Pipeline and closed-won take months. | 4. Booking in minutes to days. Job or case outcome in days to weeks (PI cases resolve in months). | 4 for lead validity (within about 72h at LSA). 2 for sale (insurance and mortgage close in weeks, solar in months). |
| **Clawback / returns** | 3. Agencies commonly replace no-shows or unqualified meetings, contract-dependent (not sourced). | 3. Can be set up as pay-on-show or pay-on-sign. | 3. Returns and credits exist but are one-sided (see LSA exclusions). |
| **Buyer auditability** | 4. Recordings, email threads, calendar. | 4. Call recordings and transcripts: an AI makes every interaction auditable. | 2. Consent and source provenance are opaque. TrustedForm and Jornaya help partially. |
| **Attribution** | 3. Multi-touch B2B makes "sourced vs influenced" fights common. | 4. Clear: the agent answered, qualified and booked. The dispute is over whether the lead would have converted anyway. | 2. Shared leads sold to 3–5 buyers. Last-touch fights. |
| **Frequency** | 4. Tens to hundreds of meetings per month per client. | 5. High volume of calls and forms. | 5. Very high volume. |
| **Stakes / liability** | 3. Low per event. Deliverability and brand damage. GDPR for the EU. | 3. TCPA on callbacks and texts. Legal intake raises unauthorized-practice and ethics concerns (not researched). | 2. TCPA and state mini-TCPA exposure of $500–1,500 per call or text, no cap, aggressive plaintiffs' bar. |
| **Would AI collapse the per-unit price?** | Partially. Cost of labor falls from about $533 to about $80–150 per meeting (aggregator), but falling reply rates and deliverability caps limit supply. Per-meeting prices likely compress toward $100–300, not to zero. | No for the outcome. The price is anchored to the value of a job or case, and AI mainly recovers leakage (unanswered calls). SaaS-priced alternatives ($500/month tools) cap how much can be captured. | No at the aggregator level: Angi's per-lead revenue is stable and demand is owned by Google and the platforms. AI lowers the cost to produce fake leads, which raises verification costs. |
| **Overall** | 3.0. Viable if priced per meeting held with an ICP-fit rubric agreed in advance. | 3.7. Best fit. | 2.4. Worst fit for a new entrant. Incumbents own demand and verification. |

- **Where AI agents can win:**
  1. **Inbound speed-to-lead and intake**, priced per booked appointment or signed case, in home services, legal (especially PI) and insurance. The buyer already pays $50–800+ per lead and loses a large share to unanswered calls. The agent's outcome is observable in the buyer's own logs.
  2. **Outbound B2B per meeting held**, a smaller but real niche. It is structurally what appointment-setting agencies already sell. AI lowers COGS, and the 11x story shows buyers want pay-for-results to avoid shelfware risk.
  3. **Vertical consumer lead gen** as a supplier is the weakest option. It requires owning demand (SEO and paid media), faces a fraud and consent-compliance burden, and competes with Google LSA, Angi and MediaAlpha.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on dispute rates per meeting in B2B pay-per-meeting contracts.
- No data on AI voice agents' actual lift in booked appointments from controlled studies.
- Unauthorized-practice and ethics limits on AI legal intake (for example, ABA Formal Opinion 512, July 2024) were not researched.
