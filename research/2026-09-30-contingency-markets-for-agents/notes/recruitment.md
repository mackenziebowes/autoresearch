# Recruitment (contingency and retained search) as a market for outcome-priced AI agents (as of 2026-09-30)

Source-quality legend: **[C]** = confirmed (primary source: company release, court or government record, SIA/ASA headline); **[R]** = reported by credible press; **[A]** = aggregator, SEO content or vendor-competitor content (directional only). Many "pricing guides" come from competitors (Pin, HeroHunt, and Paraform writing about Juicebox), and they are flagged. This builds on 2026-09-29 notes (`non_cx_outcome_pricing.md`), which cover Paraform's 20–25% contingency, its $40M Series B, Mercor's ~$2B gross, and Juicebox's seat pricing. Only new or updated facts are given here.

Research budget note: this was a ~15-call pass. Several items (Tezi, Moonhub, Findem pricing, placement volumes, non-US data) were not verified and are listed as gaps.

## Market size: US/global staffing, perm vs temp, placement volumes

### Takeaway
SIA puts US staffing at about $179–183B (2025–2026) and forecasts flat-to-low growth after the 2022 peak of $243.9B. Permanent placement ("place and search") is a small slice, around 11% or about $20B in the US. That share is an aggregator split and conflicts with other SIA/ASA numbers, so treat it as an order of magnitude. The addressable contingency-fee pool for AI agents is therefore tens of billions, not hundreds.

### Cited Findings
- SIA September 2026 update: US staffing market projected to grow 2.4% in 2026 to **$183.1B** [C, headline via search snippet; report paywalled]. — [SIA Sept 2026 update](https://www.staffingindustry.com/research/research-reports/americas/us-staffing-industry-forecast-september-2026-update)
- Earlier SIA vintage (Sept 2025): 2025 = **$178.9B**, 2026 = $183.3B (+2%) [A, via Pin]. A mid-2026 vintage (reported 2026-07-06) had 2026 at **$180.2B** (+1%), 2027 at ~$183B, against a 2022 peak of **$243.9B** and a pre-pandemic $185.5B [A, StaffingPulse summarizing SIA/ASA]. — [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/state-of-recruitment-agencies-report/); [StaffingPulse](https://www.thestaffingpulse.com/news/revenue-decline-2025.html)
- ASA/SIA 2025 full year: temporary and contract sales **$113.5B (−8.5%)**; 9.5M temp workers (−8.5%). Q1 2026 staffing sales were $27.6B, with employment down 4.6% YoY (vs −10.8% a year earlier) [A, StaffingPulse, 2026-07-06]. — [StaffingPulse](https://www.thestaffingpulse.com/news/revenue-decline-2025.html)
- Perm vs temp split: temp/contract ~89% (~$159B), **permanent placement ~11% (~$19.7B) in 2025** [A, Pin citing "SIA + ASA"]. **Conflict:** ASA reports temp/contract at $113.5B, not $159B. The ~$179B SIA total also includes other segments (e.g., travel nurse, VMS/MSP-related revenue, per SIA segmentation), so the 89/11 split is an aggregator approximation. — [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/state-of-recruitment-agencies-report/); [StaffingPulse](https://www.thestaffingpulse.com/news/revenue-decline-2025.html)
- SIA's 2026 segment growth forecasts: life sciences ~5%, engineering ~3%, finance/accounting ~2%, IT and marketing/creative ~1% [A]. — [StaffingPulse](https://www.thestaffingpulse.com/news/revenue-decline-2025.html)
- Industry structure: ~27,000 US staffing companies in ~54,000 offices (ASA, 2024–25). The top 224 firms ($100M+ each) hold $126.4B, about 67.5% of the market (SIA Largest Staffing Firms 2025) [A, via Pin]. — [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/state-of-recruitment-agencies-report/)
- Global RPO market is $8–10B (2025), projected at $16–23B by 2030 [A, unnamed "RPO Strategic Business Report"]. — [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/state-of-recruitment-agencies-report/)
- Placement speed benchmarks: commercial/light industrial 4–6 days; professional perm 32 days (agency) vs 44 (in-house); exec search 90–120 days [A, Pin citing SHRM 2025/Bullhorn GRID 2026]. — [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/state-of-recruitment-agencies-report/)
- Earlier SIA editorial forecast "place and search" revenue down 9% in 2023, which shows that perm is the most cyclical segment [C, SIA editorial]. — [SIA](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/staffing-headwinds-persist-industry-revenue-to-slip-3)

### Inferences
- At an average fee of ~20% of a ~$80–100k salary (~$16–20k per placement), a ~$20B US perm pool implies roughly 1–1.25M agency perm placements a year. This is a back-of-envelope figure, not a sourced number.
- Perm is cyclical and was in a 2023–2025 downturn. AI entrants are attacking a shrinking-then-stabilizing pool, which favors share-take stories (Paraform, Mercor) over market-growth stories.

### Gaps
- No primary SIA/ASA figure for 2025/2026 US perm-placement revenue or placement counts (SIA reports are paywalled). No verified global perm-placement figure. SIA's global staffing figure (~$650B+ in past years) was not re-verified this pass.

## Fees: contingency %, guarantees, compression

### Takeaway
US contingency remains anchored at **~20% of first-year base (range 15–25%, 25–30% for hard roles)**, with retained search at 25–35% and 60–90 day guarantees. AI marketplaces have not yet compressed the headline percentage. Paraform charges the incumbent rate and instead competes on speed and recruiter supply. The compression pressure shows up in flat-fee and subscription recruiting and in in-house AI sourcing, not in marketplace take rates.

### Cited Findings
- Contingency: 15–25% of first-year base, 20% benchmark, 25–30% for senior/hard-to-fill. Retained: 25–35% of total comp paid in thirds, with $80–100k minimums. Guarantee: **60–90 days** refund/replacement [A, multiple fee guides]. — [Pin negotiate fees](https://www.pin.com/blog/negotiate-recruiter-fees/); [Valuable Recruitment](https://valuablerecruitment.com/blog/recruitment-agency-fees); [Glozo](https://www.glozo.com/blog/recruitment-agency-fees)
- Fee table (2025, "SIA data via The Resource Company, Oct 2025"): contingency 15–25% (20% standard), retained 25–35% (30%), temp markup 25–40% (~32% on bill rate), temp-to-hire conversion 8–15% or $500–3,000 flat [A]. — [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/state-of-recruitment-agencies-report/)
- Healthcare: temp-to-perm conversion fees are now **illegal in Illinois** for nurse agencies, and Minnesota caps all nurse-agency charges (2026 RN cap $78.51/hr) [A, needs statute check]. Travel RN bill rates run ~$85–115/hr in 2026, with agency margin of 20–35% of bill rate. — [Sigma Nursing job board](https://jobs.sigmanursing.org/career-resources/employer-8/travel-nurse-agencies-vs-direct-hire-2026-124); [Boostpoint](https://boostpoint.com/industries-healthcare/nurse-staffing-agency-costs/)
- Paraform (Series B post, **2026-03-18**): 1,000+ companies; >$50M paid to recruiters to date, "projected $1B+ annually"; average placed salary **$260k** ($300–400k with equity); candidates met in ~12 days; top recruiters earn up to $300k/month. The post does not disclose fee %. [C] — [Paraform](https://www.paraform.com/blog/series-b)
- Paraform was reported to claim **>$100M annualized revenue** on its homepage (July 2026) [A]. — [Second Talent](https://www.secondtalent.com/resources/top-ai-recruiting-startups-usa/); [HeroHunt](https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/paraform-pricing-alternatives-2026/)
- Flat-fee recruiting is marketed as a cheaper alternative to contingency (2026 comparison) [A, vendor content]. — [ISG Partners](https://www.isgpartners.com/blog/flat-fee-vs-contingency-recruiting)
- Bullhorn GRID 2026 (~2,300 respondents): AI adoption in agencies went from 48% (2024) to 61% (2025), with 10% "fully embedded"; top AI adopters fill in <10 days; "agencies using AI… 3.5–4.5× more likely" to grow revenue [A, via Pin]. — [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/state-of-recruitment-agencies-report/)

### Inferences
- Paraform's numbers imply a very high-salary mix. At $260k average salary and ~20–25% fees, the average fee is ~$50–65k, and ~$100M annualized gross implies roughly 1,500–2,000 placements a year. It is a tech/exec niche, not volume hiring.
- Fee compression is more likely to come from (a) employers insourcing with cheap AI sourcing and outreach seats (Juicebox $99–199/seat; LinkedIn HA), or (b) flat-fee or subscription recruiting, than from contingency marketplaces lowering their percentage. So far, AI has raised recruiter throughput rather than lowering the price per hire.

### Gaps
- No rigorous 2024–2026 survey time series showing average contingency % falling. The claim that "fees are stable at 15–25%" rests on aggregator restatements of Bullhorn/SIA. Paraform's actual take rate (the platform's share versus the recruiter payout) is undisclosed.

The money has flowed to two different businesses. (1) Expert-data marketplaces (Mercor, micro1) grew to hundreds of millions to billions in gross revenue by supplying contractors to AI labs, not by placing permanent hires. (2) Recruiting tools (Juicebox, LinkedIn HA, Findem, Alex, HeyMilo, Paradox) sell seats, licenses or per-interview pricing. **Paraform is the only scaled, venture-backed, truly per-hire (contingency) AI-era player found**, plus a tiny newcomer, Contrario.

- Outcome pricing in recruiting is held by marketplaces that aggregate human recruiters (Paraform, Contrario). Pure-software agents price per seat or per interview, because the software alone does not own the hire outcome (hiring manager decisions, offer and acceptance).
- Workday's ~$1B Paradox purchase and LinkedIn's HA lock the high-volume and top-of-funnel layers into incumbent suites. That weakens standalone seat-priced startups and pushes independents toward outcome and services models.

## Applicant-spam arms race

### Takeaway
AI auto-apply has raised application volume sharply (≈250 applicants per posting on Greenhouse; applications per recruiter +411–412% since 2022) while recruiting teams shrank. The result is that inbound applications are becoming a low-signal channel. This favors curated sourcing, referrals and marketplace recruiters whose candidates arrive pre-vetted. It also lifts demand for AI screeners, which carry regulatory exposure.

### Cited Findings
- Greenhouse (CEO Daniel Chait, ~July 2026): ~175,000 live jobs averaging **254 applicants per posting**; applications per recruiter **+412%**; candidates can spend ~$20 on tools that auto-apply to Greenhouse jobs; he calls it a "doom loop" [R]. — [Benzinga/Yahoo Finance](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/07/60693446/job-seekers-are-using-ai-to-apply-everywhere-recruiters-are-using-ai-to-filter-them-greenhouse-ceo-calls-it-a-doom-loop)
- Greenhouse report (covered 2026-05-09): recruiters handle 411% more annual applications than in 2022, while recruiting teams are 55% smaller since 2022 [R/A]. — [RecTech Media](https://www.rectechmedia.com/blog/2026/5/9/greenhouse-report-more-applications-fewer-recruiters)
- 77% of hiring teams regularly see AI-generated or AI-assisted applications, up from 53% in early 2024 [A]. The average opening gets 242 applications with a 0.4% success rate (Business Insider data via Novorésumé, 2026-02-12) [A]. — [Interview Guys](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/the-average-job-opening-now-gets-242-applications/); [HireHub](https://www.thehirehub.ai/blog/ai-generated-job-applications)
- Greenhouse: 63% of job seekers have faced an AI interview (2026) [C, vendor survey]. — [Greenhouse](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/63-of-job-seekers-have-faced-an-ai-interview-most-havent-had-a-good-one-yet)

### Inferences
- The spam flood devalues job boards and inbound flow (Indeed/LinkedIn applicant volume) and raises the value of *verified, attributed introductions*, which is exactly what a contingency fee pays for. This is a tailwind for outcome-priced agents that can prove candidate authenticity (identity and fraud checks, live interview).
- Countermeasures (AI screeners, AI interviewers) push employers into LL144 / EU high-risk regimes and Mobley-type liability.

### Gaps
- No verified data on recruiter response rates to AI-generated applications, or on candidate-fraud prevalence (deepfake or proxy interviews) in this pass. Few primary Greenhouse/LinkedIn data releases were fetched directly.

## Underserved niches

### Takeaway
The good combination is (a) frequent hiring, (b) meaningful fee per hire, and (c) weak incumbent tech. Candidates:
- **Healthcare/nursing perm.** Chronic shortage, high travel-nurse costs create an obvious perm-conversion ROI, and there are regulatory quirks.
- **Skilled trades and licensed professions**: electricians, HVAC, CDL drivers, pharmacists, CPAs. Verifiable licenses make them good for agents.
- **Non-US markets**: EU hiring with the AI Act now delayed to Dec 2027.

Hourly/frontline hiring is high frequency but low fee, and Workday-Paradox now owns much of it, so per-hire pricing there must be a flat fee (hundreds of dollars), not a percentage.

### Cited Findings
- Frontline conversational hiring consolidated into Workday via Paradox (2025-10-01) [C]. — [Workday](https://newsroom.workday.com/2025-10-01-Workday-Completes-Acquisition-of-Paradox)
- Commercial/light-industrial fills take 4–6 days; temp-to-hire conversion fees are only 8–15% or $500–3,000 flat [A]. Light-industrial staffing firms trade at 4.0–4.5× EBITDA vs 5.5–7.0× for IT/healthcare specialists (Griffin Financial, 2025) [A]. — [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/state-of-recruitment-agencies-report/)
- Nursing: travel RN at ~$85–115/hr bill rate; a 13-week med-surg contract costs ~$54–72k (2026). Conversion fees are banned in Illinois, and Minnesota caps charges [A]. — [Sigma Nursing](https://jobs.sigmanursing.org/career-resources/employer-8/travel-nurse-agencies-vs-direct-hire-2026-124)
- SIA's 2026 fastest-growing segments are life sciences (~5%) and engineering (~3%); IT is ~1% [A]. — [StaffingPulse](https://www.thestaffingpulse.com/news/revenue-decline-2025.html)
- Paraform's current core is high-salary tech/GTM (average $260k) [C]. — [Paraform](https://www.paraform.com/blog/series-b)

### Inferences
- **Tech/exec (Paraform's lane):** big fees, low frequency, and increasingly contested by Mercor, Juicebox users and LinkedIn HA.
- **Nursing/allied health perm:** hospitals compare ~$60k per travel contract against a one-off perm fee, so the ROI is very legible. The downsides are state-level fee rules and heavy credential verification, which an agent can automate.
- **Skilled trades and licensed roles:** state license databases give a neutral verifier for candidate quality. Incumbent agencies are fragmented and local (27k US firms).
- **Hourly:** per-hire fees must be flat and low. This niche is best served by per-hire SaaS or Workday add-ons, not contingency %.
- These are reasoned inferences. No direct data on incumbent weakness per niche was found.

### Gaps
- No sourced placement fee % for nursing perm, trades or licensed professions. No non-US contingency fee data (UK/EU typically 15–25%, but not verified this pass). No data on AI entrants specifically in trades or nursing perm.

## Regulatory risk

### Takeaway
Regulatory risk is real but was delayed or softened in 2025–26. EU high-risk obligations for employment AI moved to **2 Dec 2027**. Colorado's AI Act was pared back and pushed to **1 Jan 2027**. Illinois notice rules are stalled. NYC LL144 enforcement was found "ineffective" and is now tightening. The biggest live risk is **litigation**: Mobley v. Workday is proceeding as an ADEA collective action against a *vendor*, which sets a precedent that AI vendors can be treated as agents of employers.

### Cited Findings
- **Mobley v. Workday** (N.D. Cal., 3:23-cv-00770):
  - Preliminary ADEA collective certification on **2025-05-16**, covering applicants aged 40+ since 2020-09-24.
  - Notice plan approved **2025-12-02**.
  - Workday was required to identify customers using HiredScore AI features.
  - **March 2026:** court held that ADEA disparate-impact protection covers applicants.
  - **2026-05-28:** bias-testing data may be protected by attorney-client privilege.
  - **2026-06-22:** motion to dismiss the FEHA (California) collective claims denied.
  - Case ongoing as of the latest sources. [C/R, law-firm and clearinghouse summaries] — [Clearinghouse](https://clearinghouse.net/case/44074/); [Norton Rose Fulbright, June 2026](https://www.insidetechlaw.com/blog/2026/06/behind-the-privilege-shield-safeguarding-ai-bias-testing-data-in-employment-decisions); [SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/technology/workday-ai-lawsuit-wake-up-call-hr); [AI Governance for HR](https://www.aigovernanceforhr.com/p/the-mobley-v-workday-case-didnt-end)
- **NYC LL144**: NY State Comptroller audit (2025-12-02) found DCWP enforcement "ineffective." 75% of test 311 calls were misrouted; auditors found 17 potential violations among 32 companies versus DCWP's 1. DCWP agreed to most recommendations. Penalties are $500–1,500 per day. [C] — [NY OSC](https://www.osc.ny.gov/state-agencies/audits/2025/12/02/enforcement-local-law-144-automated-employment-decision-tools); [DLA Piper, Jan 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/01/critical-audit-of-nyc-ai-hiring-law-signals-increased-risk-for-employers)
- **EU AI Act**: employment AI is Annex III high-risk. The Digital Omnibus deal was reached on 2026-05-06, confirmed by Council representatives 2026-05-13, and given final Council approval 2026-06-29. It moves high-risk employment obligations from 2 Aug 2026 to **2 Dec 2027**; most transparency obligations still applied from 2 Aug 2026 [C/R]. — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html?id=102nbon); [Ogletree](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/eu-nears-approval-of-agreement-to-delay-rules-for-ai-use-in-employment-decisions/)
- **Colorado**: SB 189, signed 2026-05-14, scales back the AI Act and delays it from 2026-06-30 to **2027-01-01** [R]. — [Law and the Workplace, May 2026](https://www.lawandtheworkplace.com/2026/05/major-developments-put-colorados-ai-law-on-ice-ahead-of-implementation/); [Seyfarth](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/artificial-intelligence-legal-roundup-colorado-postpones-implementation-of-ai-law-as-california-finalizes-new-employment-discrimination-regulations-and-illinois-disclosure-law-set-to-take-effect.html)
- **Illinois HB 3773**: effective **2026-01-01**. It amends the IHRA to bar discriminatory AI use and requires notice. IDHR notice rules were proposed 2026-05-15 and postponed 2026-06-02 [A/R]. — [AI Compliance Atlas](https://aicomplianceatlas.com/law/illinois-hb-3773); [Epstein Becker Green](https://www.ebglaw.com/insights/publications/workplace-ai-regulation-in-2026-how-employers-can-navigate-the-changing-legal-landscape)
- California finalized new employment-discrimination regulations covering automated decision systems (2025) [R]. — [Seyfarth](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/artificial-intelligence-legal-roundup-colorado-postpones-implementation-of-ai-law-as-california-finalizes-new-employment-discrimination-regulations-and-illinois-disclosure-law-set-to-take-effect.html)

### Inferences
- An outcome-priced agent that *selects or ranks* candidates is squarely an AEDT, a high-risk system and a possible "agent" of the employer under Mobley. Bias audits and adverse-impact logging are a cost of doing business. They are also a moat: incumbents like Workday face class exposure.
- A marketplace where human recruiters make the submit decision (Paraform's model) partly shields the platform. Pure autonomous screening carries the most liability.

### Gaps
- No EEOC enforcement actions against AI hiring vendors found in 2025–26 (the EEOC withdrew AI guidance in early 2025; not re-verified here). No damages or settlement figure in Mobley yet.

## Verification profile (scored 1–5, 5 = most favorable to outcome-priced agents)

### Takeaway
Recruitment is one of the best-verifiable contingency markets. The start date and payroll are a neutral event, the fee is already contingent, and buyers know the model. Its weak points are clawback exposure during the guarantee, attribution disputes (with the spam flood making "who sourced first" more contested), and discrimination liability. AI is more likely to compress fees in commodity roles than in scarce-talent roles.

### Cited Findings (basis for scores)
- Fee payable on start; 60–90 day guarantee [A]. — [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/negotiate-recruiter-fees/)
- Time-to-fill: 4–6 days (light industrial), ~32 days (professional), 90–120 days (exec) [A]. — [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/state-of-recruitment-agencies-report/)
- Liability: Mobley collective; LL144 penalties of $500–1,500 per day [C]. — [Clearinghouse](https://clearinghouse.net/case/44074/); [NY OSC](https://www.osc.ny.gov/state-agencies/audits/2025/12/02/enforcement-local-law-144-automated-employment-decision-tools)
- Application spam: ~250 per posting [R]. — [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/07/60693446/job-seekers-are-using-ai-to-apply-everywhere-recruiters-are-using-ai-to-filter-them-greenhouse-ceo-calls-it-a-doom-loop)

### Inferences (scores are analyst judgment, not sourced figures)
| Feature | Score | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral verifier | **5** | The hire start date, offer letter and payroll (W-2/HRIS) are third-party, binary events. |
| Feedback latency | **3** | Days (hourly) to 1–4 months (professional/exec) from sourcing to start, plus the guarantee period before revenue is final. |
| Clawback exposure | **3** | A 60–90 day guarantee means ~10–20% of fee revenue may be at refund or replacement risk (retention failure rate not sourced). Agents with weak candidate-fit judgment are hit hardest. |
| Buyer auditability | **4** | The buyer sees every hire and salary. It can't easily audit *counterfactuals* (would we have hired them anyway?). |
| Attribution | **2** | The classic "ownership" dispute: a candidate already in the ATS, applied directly, or also submitted by another agency. The spam flood and multi-agent sourcing worsen this. Marketplaces solve it with timestamped submissions and 6–12 month ownership windows (industry convention, not sourced here). |
| Frequency | **3** (hourly 5, exec 1) | Varies by segment. Perm professional hiring is episodic per employer, while staffing and frontline are continuous. |
| Stakes/liability | **2** | Discrimination law (ADEA, Title VII, FEHA, IHRA), LL144, EU high-risk. The vendor-as-agent theory in Mobley is live. |
| Fee-collapse risk | **2–3** | In commodity roles, cheap sourcing seats (Juicebox $99–199; LinkedIn HA) let employers insource and pressure the 20% fee. In scarce-talent roles (Paraform avg $260k salaries), fees have held. The overall score is weighted toward commodity roles. |

- **Bottom line (inference):** AI agents *can* win contingency recruiting, most plausibly as (1) marketplaces or AI-augmented agencies that own the candidate relationship and keep the 20% fee while cutting cost per placement (a margin story, not a price-cut story), or (2) flat-fee per-hire offerings in high-volume licensed or frontline niches where incumbents are fragmented. Pure autonomous agents that pick candidates face the most liability and attribution friction. The largest "AI recruiting" revenue so far (Mercor, micro1) comes from lab data labor, not perm fees, and should not be counted as proof that the perm market has been captured.
