# Tax Credit Consultancy (US R&D credit + WOTC, 179D/45L, cost segregation, ERC) as a Market for Outcome-Priced AI Agents

Research date: 2026-09-30. Status labels: **[confirmed]** = primary/government source; **[reported]** = press, professional firm or company statement; **[aggregator]** = Tracxn/CB Insights/Crunchbase/ZoomInfo/SEO blogs, or a search-engine summary I could not trace to a primary source. About 22 tool calls were used; several quantitative gaps are flagged below.

## 1. Market size: credits claimed, dollar value, consulting market, non-claimers

### Takeaway
The federal R&D credit is one of the largest corporate tax expenditures. JCT puts it at about $189B over FY2025–2029, roughly $38B a year. The IRS's public SOI Form 6765 tables stop at tax year 2014 ($12.6B claimed), so there is no official, current count of claimants or dollars. The consulting market size and the number of eligible non-claimers are unmeasured in public sources.

### Cited Findings
- The IRS SOI "Corporation research credit" page offers Form 6765 tables for 2001–2014 only. The newest is Table 1 for TY2014, and most other tables stop at TY2013. The page was last reviewed 2025-11-09. It excludes 1120S, REIT and RIC returns. **[confirmed]** — [IRS SOI](https://www.irs.gov/statistics/soi-tax-stats-corporation-research-credit)
- CRS report R48848 (2026-02-06) uses 2014 as the most recent data. Total credits claimed were **$12.6B**. C corporations made 98% of claims. The Alternative Simplified Credit accounted for $7.8B (73%). Firms with $250M+ in receipts were 14% of claimants but took 85% of credit value (2013). By sector: manufacturing 59%, information 17%, professional/scientific/technical 10% (2014). **[confirmed, via CRS]** — [CRS R48848 (EveryCRSReport mirror)](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R48848.html)
- JCT tax expenditure estimate, cited by CRS (Feb 2026): the research credit costs **$188.9B in FY2025–2029**, second among corporate tax expenditures. R&E expensing costs $104.1B over the same period. **[confirmed, via CRS]** — [CRS R48848](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R48848.html)
- A search-engine summary said "in tax year 2021, U.S. corporations claimed over $32 billion in R&D tax credits." I could not trace it to a specific SOI table. **[aggregator, unverified]** — [search surfaced alongside SOI Bulletin Summer 2025](https://www.irs.gov/statistics/soi-tax-stats-soi-bulletin-summer-2025)
- Historical SOI: TY2005 credit was $6.4B (71.2% to manufacturing) and TY2004 was $5.6B. **[confirmed]** — [IRS SOI paper](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-soi/04-05crreac.pdf)
- Small-business payroll offset: TIGTA found that in TY2017 over 2,200 qualified small businesses claimed about $53M against payroll tax. 143 of them claimed or received about $11.8M in erroneous credits (reported 2019). **[reported, citing TIGTA]** — [RSM](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2019/audit-identifies-millions-in-erroneous-rd-payroll-tax-credit-cla.html)
- The payroll tax offset for qualified small businesses (under $5M gross receipts, 5 years or fewer of receipts) is now up to $500K a year against Social Security and Medicare. Fondo reports that most startups use this route, which makes the credit effectively refundable in cash (2025–2026). **[reported]** — [Fondo](https://fondo.com/tax-credits)
- Consultancy size proxies: alliantgroup is estimated at **$297.8M annual revenue and 2,700 employees** (Houston, founded 2002, backed by Quilvest). **[aggregator: ZoomInfo/PitchBook]** — [ZoomInfo](https://www.zoominfo.com/c/alliantgroup-lp/1310794); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/12245-95). Leyton (Paris, founded 1997) has about 3,000 employees across 17 locations (2025), with US hubs in Boston, Houston, NYC and Phoenix. **[aggregator]** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyton_(company))

### Inferences
- About $38B a year of credits (JCT) is a rough ceiling. If consultants touch only 10–20% of credit dollars at 15–30% fees, the fee pool would be roughly $0.6–2.3B a year. **This is my illustrative arithmetic, not a sourced market size.** Most credit value goes to large corporations that use Big 4 or in-house teams on hourly or fixed fees. The contingency segment is mostly mid-market and SMB/startup.
- The large-firm concentration (85% of value to 14% of claimants) points to two separate markets. One is a low-count, high-value enterprise market, which is sticky and served by the Big 4. The other is a high-count, low-ticket SMB/startup market. The second is where contingency pricing and software or agent entrants play.

### Gaps
- No official post-2014 SOI claimant counts or dollar totals were found. The SOI Bulletin or JCT/Treasury may have newer internal figures, but none were located.
- I found no credible third-party estimate (IBISWorld or similar) of the US R&D credit consulting market's revenue.
- I found no reliable estimate of eligible non-claiming companies. Vendor claims like "most eligible SMBs don't claim" are marketing and unsourced.

## 2. Fees: contingency vs flat, timing, audit clawbacks, trends

### Takeaway
Contingency fees typically run **15–30% of the credit**, with boutiques up to 35–40%. Fintech and payroll platforms have pushed toward **15–20%** (Gusto "up to 15%", Pilot 20%). Fixed-fee studies coexist. Audit exposure falls on the taxpayer. How much of the fee comes back on disallowance depends on the contract, and I found no standard.

### Cited Findings
- Reported contingency ranges vary by source:
  - 25–40% "industry standard" — [Swanson Reed](https://www.swansonreed.com/research-tax-credit/faqs/what-is-the-standard-contingency-fee-for-rd-tax-credit-advisors/)
  - 10–30%, with 15–20% common for straightforward claims; the same guide says 30% is at the extreme high end and signals aggressiveness — [Corporate Tax Advisors](https://corporatetaxadvisors.com/rd-tax-credit-consultants-complete-guide-to-choosing-expert-advisory-services/)
  - 20–30% in 2026 — [Claimship](https://www.claimship.com/rd-tax-credit-study-cost)
  - **[reported/aggregator; vendor blogs, 2025–2026]**
- Pilot charges **20% of the R&D credit received** (current pricing page, accessed 2026-09). **[reported]** — [Pilot](https://pilot.com/rd-credit)
- Gusto's R&D credit service charges "up to 15%" of the client's credit. Gusto also offers real-time credit access for a $100 monthly fee (accessed 2026-09). **[reported]** — [Gusto](https://gusto.com/accountants/r-and-d-tax-credit-service)
- The Canadian SR&ED market shows the same flat-fee vs contingency debate. **[aggregator]** — [Glauq](https://glauq.com/blog/sred-consultant-fees)
- On timing: for startups using the payroll offset, the credit is elected on Form 6765 with the income tax return. It is then applied quarterly against payroll taxes on Form 8974, starting the quarter after the return is filed. Cash therefore arrives 3–15 months after the tax year. **[reported]** — [Neo.Tax on Form 8974](https://www.neo.tax/blog/how-to-file-your-form-8974-with-gusto)

### Inferences
- Contingency fees are usually due when the credit is "secured": when the return is filed, or as payroll offsets are realized. That can be years before the audit statute (3 years, or longer for carryforwards) closes. Unless the contract includes an audit-defense or refund clause, the consultant keeps the fee on later disallowance. That is the core misalignment in this market.
- Platform entrants priced at 15–20% have already compressed fees from the boutique 25–40% range. An AI-native player would likely need to go below about 10% or a flat fee to differentiate.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative survey of fee clawback terms, such as what share of contracts refund fees on IRS disallowance.
- I found no dated time series of fee trends. The compression claim rests on current pricing pages only.

## 3. Incumbents

### Takeaway
The market is fragmented. It has three tiers: Big 4 and national CPA firms for enterprise; large specialist boutiques (alliantgroup, Leyton, Source Advisors, KBKG, Swanson Reed, Exactera) for the mid-market, often on contingency; and payroll or fintech platforms for startups.

### Cited Findings
- alliantgroup: about $298M revenue and 2,700 staff. Its services span the R&D credit, 179D and others. **[aggregator]** — [ZoomInfo](https://www.zoominfo.com/c/alliantgroup-lp/1310794); [alliantgroup services](https://www.alliantgroup.com/services/)
- Leyton: about 3,000 employees globally (2025). Its US practice covers R&D credits, cost segregation and tariff advisory. **[aggregator]** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyton_(company))
- Source Advisors: CB Insights lists a PE investment on 2023-04-03 and an acquisition on 2024-07-12, with details not visible. **[aggregator, low confidence]** — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/sourcehov-tax/financials)
- Big 4 and national firms (PwC, Grant Thornton, BDO, RSM, Plante Moran, Baker Tilly and others) publish heavily on Form 6765 and Section 174A. They are the default for large corporates. **[reported]** — [PwC](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/services/tax/library/pwc-new-timelines-for-form-6765-provide-welcome-relief.html); [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.com/insights/alerts/tax/2025/insights/new-form-reporting-requirements)

### Inferences
- The Big 4 moat in enterprise is audit defense, controversy work and the relationship, not preparation labor. AI is most disruptive in the mid-market and SMB tiers, where the value delivered is mostly data gathering and narrative drafting.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable market-share data or revenue figures for Leyton US, Source Advisors, KBKG or Exactera.

## 4. AI and tech entrants

### Takeaway
The first wave of "software plus contingency" R&D credit startups (MainStreet, Neo.Tax, Boast, Fondo) raised modest capital. MainStreet, the best funded, collapsed into a small acqui-hire by Employer.com in 2025. The surviving model is the credit bundled as a feature inside payroll, accounting or back-office platforms (Gusto, Pilot, Warp with Fondo) at 15–20% fees. I found no clearly AI-native, well-funded 2025–2026 entrant.

### Cited Findings
- **MainStreet** (San Jose, founded 2019) took a cut of the credits it found. It laid off about 30% of staff in 2022, citing "an incredibly rough market." It was **acquired by Employer.com in May 2025**, with its 15-person team joining. By July 2026 its original site no longer showed the R&D product. **[reported/aggregator]** — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3003322); [Claimship](https://www.claimship.com/vs/mainstreet); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/mainstreet-1). It integrates with Gusto payroll. **[aggregator: Tracxn]** — [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/neo.tax/__2_Z6e2uC2OgPmrXLkeeZEnfD2mzyEkoJT0VqHpAeEGw); [Gusto integration](https://gusto.com/product/integrations/neo_tax)
- **Boast** (founded 2011, Canada/US) raised a **$23M Series A led by Radian Capital**. The PR Newswire release ID suggests about 2021, but a search summary dated it to May 2025, so the date is unverified. - Smaller AI-branded tools exist, including TaxCredit.ai, CodeROI (Section G tooling from code repositories), Claimship (a comparison site) and Strike Tax. **[aggregator]** — [TaxCredit.ai](https://taxcredit.ai/); [CodeROI](https://www.coderoi.com/section-g)

### Inferences
- MainStreet's trajectory suggests that customer acquisition cost plus IRS-driven quality risk plus small ticket sizes (about $21K average credit, so about $3–4K in fees at 15–20%) do not support a venture-scale standalone contingency business. Distribution through payroll or ledger platforms has won.
- Section G's business-component reporting (see Section 5) creates a new "evidence from engineering systems" wedge, such as git, Jira and cloud cost data. That favors agent-style tools that read those systems, as CodeROI pitches.

### Gaps
- MainStreet's total funding and the Employer.com deal price could not be verified this session.
- I found no dated 2025–2026 funding rounds for AI-native R&D credit agents. The search returned nothing relevant.
- I found no traction metrics (ARR, customer counts) for Neo.Tax or Gusto's R&D feature.

## 5. Regulatory and verification risk

### Takeaway
Since 2021 the IRS has steadily raised the evidentiary bar for R&D claims. The steps are the five-items rule for refund claims (Oct 2021), the redesigned Form 6765 (Section G optional for TY2025, required for TY2026 for most larger filers), and "Dirty Dozen" listings since 2019. Circular 230 changes proposed in Dec 2024 would ban contingent fees on original and amended returns and refund claims, but were not final as of the latest sources found. The ERC shows how a contingency-fee mill ends: moratorium, disallowances, voluntary disclosure, a statutory cutoff, promoter penalties and prison sentences. OBBBA (July 4, 2025) restored domestic R&E expensing (§174A), including retroactive relief for small businesses. That increases incentive to file amended returns, which is exactly the refund-claim channel under scrutiny.

### Cited Findings
**Documentation for refund claims since 2021/2022**
- In October 2021 the IRS, with Chief Counsel memo 20214101F, required **five items** for a valid research credit refund claim:
  1. All business components.
  2. The research activities for each component.
  3. Who performed them.
  4. The information each person sought to discover.
  5. Total qualified wages, supplies and contract research.
- A 45-day "perfection" window was allowed through 2025-01-10. After that, deficient claims are rejected without consideration.
- **[confirmed]** — [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/required-information-for-a-valid-research-credit-claim-for-refund); [IRS FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/corporations/research-credit-claims-section-41-on-amended-returns-frequently-asked-questions); [BDO](https://www.bdo.com/insights/tax/irs-updates-requirements-for-r-d-tax-credit-refund-claims)

**Form 6765 Section G**
- Form 6765 instructions (Rev. Dec 2025): Section G (business-component detail) is **optional for tax years beginning before 2026 and required after 2025**. Filers must report components making up at least 80% of QREs, up to 50 components.
- Exemptions cover qualified small businesses making the payroll election. They also cover filers with QREs of $1.5M or less and gross receipts of $50M or less, at the controlled-group level, on an original return.
- **[confirmed]** — [IRS i6765](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i6765); [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.com/insights/alerts/tax/2025/insights/new-form-reporting-requirements); [Schneider Downs](https://schneiderdowns.com/our-thoughts-on/rd-tax-credit-irs-section-g-2025-feedback/)

**Circular 230 §10.27**
- The current §10.27 generally bars contingent fees for matters before the IRS. Exceptions include services tied to IRS examination of an original return, or of an amended return or refund claim filed before written notice of examination or within 120 days after it. **[reported]** — [Tax Notes](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/proposed-regulations/new-proposed-regs-issued-circular-230-rules/7pg68)
- In *Ridgely v. Lew* (D.D.C. 2014), the court held that Circular 230's contingent-fee bar cannot reach preparing ordinary refund claims. It applies only once the relationship with the IRS is adversarial. This is why contingency R&D amended-return work has been widespread. **[reported]** — [Iowa State CALT](https://www.calt.iastate.edu/article/circular-230%E2%80%99s-bar-contingent-fees-only-applies-once-taxpayer%E2%80%99s-relationship-irs-becomes)
- Proposed regulations published 2024-12-26 would remove §10.27 and make contingent fees for preparing **original or amended returns or refund claims** "disreputable conduct." They would move the rule into a new Subpart C that applies to all practitioners and withdraw the 2009 proposal. Comments closed 2025-02-24, with a hearing on 2025-03-06. **[confirmed]** — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/12/26/2024-29371/regulations-governing-practice-before-the-internal-revenue-service); [CPA Journal, Jul 2025](https://www.cpajournal.com/2025/07/30/treasury-issues-proposed-regulations-amending-circular-230-2/)
- Commentators expected final regulations "not until at least 2026." I found no evidence of finalization by 2026-09-30. **[reported]** — [Federal Tax Updates](https://federaltaxupdates.com/50)

**Promoter scrutiny**
- Aggressive R&D credit marketing has appeared on the IRS "Dirty Dozen" list since 2019. **[reported]** — [Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/opinion/irs-lists-r-d-tax-credit-among-its-dirty-dozen-so-is-it-still-safe-to-claim)
- Research credit refund claims have long been a Tier I / top compliance issue for LB&I, and the IRS publishes a Research Credit Claims Audit Techniques Guide. **[confirmed]** — [IRS RCCATG](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/research-credit-claims-audit-techniques-guide-rccatg-credit-for-increasing-research-activities-section-41)

**ERC as the cautionary tale**
- The IRS imposed a processing **moratorium on ERC claims filed on or after 2023-09-14**. **[confirmed]** — [IRS ERC FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/coronavirus/frequently-asked-questions-about-the-employee-retention-credit)
- IRS tallies, as reported, include:
  - 28,000 disallowance letters, saving about $5B.
  - 460 criminal cases worth over $7B. An earlier figure (as of 2024-02-29) was 386 cases worth $3B.
  - Over 7,300 entities withdrew $677M through the withdrawal program.
  - The Voluntary Disclosure Program drew more than 2,600 applications disclosing $1.09B. The first VDP required repayment of 80%, the second (closed 2024-11-22) 85%.
  - **[reported, citing IRS]** — [EisnerAmper](https://www.eisneramper.com/insights/tax/irs-provides-updates-on-efforts-to-combat-erc-fraud-via-various-programs/); [Drake](https://www.drakesoftware.com/taxing-subjects/erc-fraud-victims-voluntary-disclosure-program/)
- OBBBA §70605 (enacted 2025-07-04):
  - Q3/Q4 2021 ERC claims filed after 2024-01-31 and unpaid by 2025-07-04 can no longer be allowed. **[confirmed]** — [IRS FS-2025-07, 2025-10-22](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-frequently-asked-questions-faqs-address-employee-retention-credits-under-erc-compliance-provisions-of-the-one-big-beautiful-bill)
  - The assessment statute is extended to **6 years** for those quarters, which puts IRS review into about 2030.
  - A new "COVID-ERTC promoter" category covers anyone charging refund-based fees above gross-receipt thresholds, with a **$1,000 per-failure due-diligence penalty**.
  - **[reported: RSM, 2025-07-30]** — [RSM](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2025/what-you-need-to-know-about-obbba-and-the-employee-retention-tax-credit.html). One search summary claimed the extension runs only to 2027-04-15. That conflicts with RSM, and RSM's six-year reading is more specific.
- Criminal sentences, as reported:
  - Nevada: 54 months and $26M restitution for more than 1,200 false returns.
  - New Jersey: 12 years and $55M restitution for about 1,900 returns and about $170M in claimed credits.
  - Lakisha Pearson: 52 months and $15.9M restitution.
  - Promoter indictments leave the business owner still liable (2026-05-21).
  - **[reported: law-firm blogs; DOJ primaries not fetched]** — [Frazier Law, 2026-05-21](https://www.crfrazierlaw.com/blog/when-the-erc-promoter-is-indicted-the-business-owner-is-still-on-the-hook/); [NY criminal attorneys](https://www.nyccriminalattorneys.com/what-happens-when-your-erc-claim-becomes-a-criminal-investigation/)

**Section 174 / OBBBA**
- OBBBA created **§174A**, permanently restoring immediate expensing of **domestic** R&E for tax years beginning after 2024-12-31. Foreign R&E stays on 15-year amortization.
- Small businesses with average gross receipts of $31M or less (2022–2024) may apply it **retroactively to 2022–2024 by amending returns**.
- All taxpayers may deduct unamortized 2022–2024 domestic R&E in 2025 or spread it over 2025–2026.
- **[reported: Big 4 / national CPA, Jul 2025–Apr 2026]** — [Plante Moran](https://www.plantemoran.com/explore-our-thinking/insight/2025/07/obbb-restores-expensing-of-domestic-section-174-re-costs); [Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.com/insights/domestic-research-expenditures-section-174-under-obbba); [EisnerAmper](https://www.eisneramper.com/insights/technology/section-174a-rd-expensing-deduction-strategy-0426/)

**Adjacent incentives**
- **179D**: OBBBA ends it for property whose construction begins after **2026-06-30**. **[reported]** — [CTI](https://ctillc.com/blog/cost-seg-179d-itc-stacking-strategy/)
- **45L**: I recall a similar OBBBA sunset for homes acquired after 2026-06-30, but this was not verified this session and is listed under Gaps.
- **Cost segregation**: OBBBA permanently restored **100% bonus depreciation** for property acquired after 2025-01-19. That is a tailwind for cost segregation studies. **[reported]** — [Cherry Bekaert](https://www.cbh.com/insights/articles/trumps-tax-bill-bonus-depreciation-cost-segregation/)
- **WOTC**: it **expired 2025-12-31** and had not been reauthorized as of early Sept 2026. S.3265 / H.R.6231 would extend it through 2030. It has historically been revived retroactively. **[confirmed via CRS, updated 2026-05-13; status per vendor blogs]** — [CRS R43729](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R43729/R43729.9.pdf); [UHY](https://uhy-us.com/insights/news/2025/december/work-opportunity-tax-credit-wotc-expiration-extension-and-what-employers-need-to-know)

**UK comparison (HMRC crackdown)**
- The error and fraud rate in UK R&D relief fell to **5.9% (£481M) in 2024–25**, from 7.8% in 2023–24 and 17.6% in 2021–22. The SME scheme was 10.6% (£339M).
- Claimant companies fell from about 86,000 (2021) to about 44,065 (2024). Claims fell 26% year on year.
- Compliance checks covered 17% of claims, and **77% of checked claims were adjusted**. £441M was found incorrectly claimed in 2023–24.
- **[reported, citing HMRC]** — [ForrestBrown](https://forrestbrown.co.uk/news/hmrc-annual-report-r-and-d-tax-analysis/); [EmpowerRD](https://www.empowerrd.com/rd-tax-credit-statistics-2025-analysis/); [BDO UK](https://www.bdo.co.uk/en-gb/news/2025/is-hmrcs-progress-in-tackling-fraud-and-error-in-r-d-claims-actually-a-success)

**Canada**
- SR&ED has a comparable contingency consultant market. **[aggregator]** — [Glauq](https://glauq.com/blog/sred-consultant-fees)

### Inferences
- The post-2021 IRS trajectory is: evidence per business component, named individuals, and the uncertainty each sought to resolve. That turns the product from "calculate a number" into "produce an auditable evidence file." This favors agents plugged into source systems (HRIS, git, tickets). It makes a thin "questionnaire plus percentage" model riskier.
- If the Circular 230 proposals are finalized, **Circular 230 practitioners** (CPAs, EAs, attorneys) could no longer charge contingent fees on original or amended returns or refund claims. Unenrolled non-practitioner software firms might sit outside, although the new Subpart C was drafted to reach all practitioners. Outcome pricing for credentialed firms could be pushed toward flat or subscription fees. That is a structural opening for software sold on flat or SaaS pricing.
- §174A retroactivity makes 2022–2024 amended returns attractive to small businesses for both §174A and §41 claims. That is a near-term demand spike, but in the exact refund-claim channel the IRS polices hardest.
- The UK example shows the likely end state of a crackdown. Claim counts roughly halve, adjustment rates on checked claims are very high, and weak contingency boutiques exit.

### Gaps
- No R&D-credit-specific audit rate or time-to-resolution data were found in public IRS or TIGTA sources this session. Anecdotally, audits run 1–3+ years, but that is unsourced.
- The 45L sunset date is unverified.
- No DOJ press releases specific to R&D credit promoters (as opposed to ERC) were found.
- The final status of Circular 230 as of 2026-09-30 is unconfirmed. There was no evidence of a final rule.
- The number of refund claims rejected under the five-items rule has not been published in the sources found.

## 6. Verification profile for outcome-priced AI agents (scored 1–5; 5 = most favorable to outcome pricing)

### Takeaway
There is a real, neutral verifier: the IRS or HMRC accepts or pays the credit. But it is slow and asymmetric. "Payment" on filing is not final, and adverse verification (audit) can come 3–6+ years later. The liability sits with the taxpayer, who is also the fee payer. That makes the R&D credit a **medium-fit** contingency market for agents. It works best where the agent also carries audit defense, holds back part of the fee or gives a refund guarantee, and prices low enough that the fee is not the story.

### Scores (inferences drawn from the cited findings above)

| Feature | Score | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral verifier | 4 | The IRS is an independent verifier. The payroll offset pays in cash within quarters, and it rejects deficient refund claims under the five-items rule ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/required-information-for-a-valid-research-credit-claim-for-refund)). But acceptance on filing is not validation. Only an exam, or the statute closing, is final. |
| Feedback latency | 2 | Initial signal (return accepted, payroll offset applied) comes in 3–15 months. The true outcome (audit or statute expiry) takes about 3 years, and 6 years for ERC Q3/Q4 2021 under OBBBA ([RSM](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2025/what-you-need-to-know-about-obbba-and-the-employee-retention-tax-credit.html)). |
| Clawback exposure | 2 | The taxpayer repays the credit plus interest and penalties. ERC VDP repayments ran 80–85% ([Drake](https://www.drakesoftware.com/taxing-subjects/erc-fraud-victims-voluntary-disclosure-program/)). HMRC adjusted 77% of checked claims ([ForrestBrown](https://forrestbrown.co.uk/news/hmrc-annual-report-r-and-d-tax-analysis/)). Consultant fee clawback is contractual and not standard. |
| Buyer auditability | 3 | The buyer can see the dollar outcome but cannot easily judge whether the claim is defensible. Section G and the five items make the evidence file more inspectable ([IRS i6765](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i6765)). |
| Attribution | 4 | The credit claimed is directly attributable to the preparer's work. The main dispute is whether it was "found money" or overclaiming, not who produced it. |
| Frequency/volume | 3 | Filing is annual per company, with quarterly payroll offsets. There are many SMB claimants but small tickets (Fondo reports an average of about $21K, per [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/H3v-fondo-automatically-get-cash-back-from-the-irs-average-startup-gets-21-000)). Enterprise volume is concentrated ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R48848.html)). |
| Stakes/liability | 2 | Penalties and interest fall on the taxpayer. Promoters face OBBBA ERC penalties, Dirty Dozen scrutiny and criminal exposure in fraud cases. A reputational blow-up can end a vendor (the MainStreet trajectory). |
| AI collapses the fee? | Yes, partly (4) | Preparation labor (interviews, narratives, payroll mapping) is highly automatable, and platforms already sit at 15–20% versus boutique 25–40%. Audit defense and professional judgment are not collapsed. Circular 230 reform, if finalized, would push credentialed firms off contingency in any case. |

### Inferences
- The best agent strategy is probably to **price low and decouple from the credit amount**: flat, SaaS or a small percentage, with a holdback or escrow until the statute closes or exam risk is priced. Pure contingency invites both the ERC-style regulatory framing ("fee based on the amount of refund" is the definition of an ERC promoter under OBBBA) and a perverse incentive to maximize claims.
- Distribution matters more than model quality. The survivors are payroll and ledger platforms, so an agent vendor likely wins as an embedded engine (as Neo.Tax is in Gusto) or by owning the evidence layer for Section G.
- Adjacent incentives rank differently:
  - Cost segregation has a strong tailwind from 100% bonus depreciation and an engineering-heavy, automatable study.
  - 179D and 45L face a mid-2026 sunset, so the window is closing.
  - WOTC is lapsed. It is high-volume and low-ticket screening that is very automatable, but the program is currently off.
  - ERC is closed and now mainly a liability and defense market.

### Gaps
- I found no empirical data on consultant-prepared vs self-prepared claim disallowance rates, which would let an agent's accuracy be priced.
- No insurance products (tax-credit insurance) pricing R&D audit risk were researched this session.
